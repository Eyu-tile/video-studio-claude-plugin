# De-escalating angry customers
id: com-deescalation
type: guide
category: difficult
audience: internal
tags: angry customer, de-escalation, complaint, upset, shouting, የተናደደ ደንበኛ, calm down, abusive customer
related: com-empathy-phrases, com-saying-no, com-threats-legal-media, com-what-never-to-say, sop-buyer-seller-disputes, sop-escalation
templates: tpl-acknowledge-complaint, tpl-delayed-response-apology, tpl-escalation-notification
policy_pending: yes

## Summary
A practical method (Listen, Acknowledge, Apologize, Act) to calm angry customers in writing and on the phone, what to avoid, and how to handle abuse while protecting yourself.

## Overview
Customers who are angry are usually afraid: of losing money, of missing an opportunity, of being ignored. Anger is often made worse by waiting, repeating their story, or receiving a template that ignores what they said. Our goal is not to win the argument but to lower the temperature enough to solve the problem.

## Guidelines
### The LAAA method
1. **Listen** — let them finish (on the phone) or read the whole message (in writing). Identify the real problem and the real worry beneath the anger.
2. **Acknowledge** — name their feeling and the situation specifically: "You paid and still have no confirmation, and your session is tomorrow. I understand why you are upset."
3. **Apologize** — for the experience, sincerely, once. "I'm sorry this has happened." Do not apologize with excuses.
4. **Act** — state the concrete action you are taking now and the next step. Ownership calms people more than words.

### Techniques
- **Lower your pace and volume** on the phone; write short, calm sentences in chat.
- **Use their name** and refer to their specific case, so they know a person is reading.
- **Don't argue facts in the first reply.** First acknowledge, then clarify.
- **Give choices** where you can (call back vs. Telegram update), which returns a sense of control.
- **Avoid trigger words:** "calm down", "policy", "as I said", "unfortunately" at the start of every sentence.
- **Raise priority** when anger comes from repeated contacts or long waits, and tell the Supervisor.
- **Offer a supervisor** if they ask, or if you have tried and the conversation is not moving.

### Abuse
- Customers may be angry; they may not insult, threaten or harass staff.
- First, a calm boundary: "I want to help you, and I can do that if we keep the conversation respectful."
- If abuse continues, end the conversation politely, inform the Supervisor, and log it in the ticket. The case still gets handled — by you or someone else.
- Threats of violence → Trust & Safety and Management immediately (see *Customers threatening legal action, media or social posts*).

## Examples
### Angry Telegram message: "This is a scam! I paid and you stole my money!!!"
**EN:** "Hello {{customer_name}}, I'm sorry — I understand how upsetting it is to pay and not see your booking. I want to sort this out with you now. Please send me the transaction reference from your payment SMS, and I will check it immediately. Your case number is {{ticket_id}}."

**AM:** "ሰላም {{customer_name}}፣ ይቅርታ — ከፍለው ቀጠሮዎን ሳያዩ ሲቀሩ ምን ያህል እንደሚያበሳጭ ይገባኛል። ይህን ጉዳይ አሁኑኑ አብረን ልንፈታው እፈልጋለሁ። እባክዎ ከክፍያ ኤስኤምኤስዎ ላይ ያለውን የግብይት መለያ ቁጥር ይላኩልኝ፤ ወዲያውኑ አጣራለሁ። የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው።"

### Customer who contacted us several times
**EN:** "{{customer_name}}, I'm sorry you have had to contact us several times about this. That should not have happened. I have read your full history, so you don't need to repeat anything. I have raised your case as a priority and I am personally following it."

**AM:** "{{customer_name}}፣ በዚህ ጉዳይ ብዙ ጊዜ ማግኘት ስላስፈለገዎት ይቅርታ እንጠይቃለን። ይህ መሆን አልነበረበትም። ሙሉ ታሪኩን አንብቤዋለሁ፤ ስለዚህ ምንም ነገር ድጋሚ መናገር አያስፈልግዎትም። ጉዳይዎን በቅድሚያ እንዲታይ አድርጌዋለሁ፤ በግሌም እየተከታተልኩት ነው።"

### Setting a boundary
**EN:** "I want to help you resolve this, and I can do that best if we keep our conversation respectful. Let's focus on your payment so I can check it for you."

**AM:** "ይህን ጉዳይ እንዲፈታ ልረዳዎት እፈልጋለሁ፤ ንግግራችንን በመከባበር ብንቀጥል በተሻለ መርዳት እችላለሁ። እስኪ ላጣራልዎት በክፍያዎ ላይ እናተኩር።"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge the specific situation | "I understand your frustration" with nothing specific |
| Apologize once, sincerely | Over-apologize or apologize with excuses |
| Tell them what you are doing now | Explain policy before acknowledging feelings |
| Offer a supervisor when needed | Refuse to escalate out of pride |
| Set a calm boundary on abuse | Insult back, threaten, or go silent |
| Log abusive behaviour factually | Write emotional comments in internal notes |

## Policy Pending Approval
- Approve the official procedure for abusive customers (warnings, ending conversations, blocking on channels, account restrictions) and who may decide each step.

## Internal Notes
- After a hard conversation, take a short break if you can and tell your Supervisor; difficult calls affect the next ones.
- Anger directed at Byfluence is not personal. Abuse directed at you personally is not acceptable — you are allowed to end it.

# Saying no / delivering bad news
id: com-saying-no
type: guide
category: difficult
audience: internal
tags: saying no, bad news, refusal, decline, rejection, አይቻልም, refund declined, not available
related: com-deescalation, com-empathy-phrases, com-what-never-to-say, pol-policy-pending-procedure, sop-refund-requests, com-tone-voice
templates: tpl-escalation-notification, tpl-status-update, tpl-acknowledge-complaint
policy_pending: no

## Summary
How to say no or deliver bad news clearly and kindly — a decision that was made, a service that isn't available, information we cannot share — while offering what we can do.

## Overview
Agents often avoid saying no clearly, which leads to vague messages, false hope and repeated contacts. A clear, respectful "no" with a reason and an alternative is better service than a "maybe". Important: you may only deliver a "no" that is actually decided (by policy, by an authorized decision or by facts such as Feature Status). If a policy is pending, the answer is "it will be reviewed", not "no".

## Guidelines
### Structure: the "Thank – No – Why – What we can do – Next" pattern
1. **Thank / acknowledge** — "Thank you for your patience while we reviewed this."
2. **Clear answer** — say it early and plainly; don't bury it.
3. **Why** — a short, honest reason in plain words (no blaming, no internal jargon).
4. **What we can do** — alternatives that are actually approved or available.
5. **Next step** — what the customer can do now; how to reach us; case number.

### Rules
- Never deliver a "no" you invented. Check that the decision was made by authorized personnel or follows an approved rule/Feature Status.
- Never deliver a "yes" you invented either — partial hope is still a promise.
- Don't hide behind "policy" without explaining it in plain words.
- If the customer disagrees, acknowledge it, and offer to have the decision reviewed if an appeal route exists (ask the Supervisor).

## Examples
### Refund request declined (decision made by authorized team)
**EN:** "Hello {{customer_name}}, thank you for your patience while our team reviewed your request (case {{ticket_id}}). After reviewing the booking and the information from both sides, the team has decided that a refund cannot be provided for this session, because the session took place as booked. I understand this is not the answer you hoped for. If you have new information about the session that we have not seen, please send it and I will make sure it is reviewed."

**AM:** "ሰላም {{customer_name}}፣ ቡድናችን ጥያቄዎን (ጉዳይ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}}) እስኪገመግም ድረስ ስለታገሱ እናመሰግናለን። ቀጠሮውን እና ከሁለቱም ወገኖች የቀረበውን መረጃ ከገመገመ በኋላ፣ ክፍለ-ጊዜው እንደተያዘው ስለተካሄደ ለዚህ ክፍለ-ጊዜ ገንዘብ መመለስ እንደማይቻል ቡድኑ ወስኗል። ይህ የጠበቁት መልስ እንዳልሆነ ይገባኛል። እኛ ያላየነው ስለ ክፍለ-ጊዜው አዲስ መረጃ ካለዎት እባክዎ ይላኩልን፤ እንዲገመገም አደርጋለሁ።"

### Feature not available (Feature Status not live)
**EN:** "Thank you for your interest, {{customer_name}}. Delivery of physical products is not available on Byfluence at the moment. Byfluence currently focuses on consultations. We will share news through our official channels when new services become available."

**AM:** "{{customer_name}}፣ ስለ ፍላጎትዎ እናመሰግናለን። በአሁኑ ጊዜ በByfluence ላይ የዕቃ ማድረስ (delivery) አገልግሎት የለም። Byfluence በአሁኑ ወቅት በምክክር አገልግሎቶች ላይ ያተኩራል። አዳዲስ አገልግሎቶች ሲጀመሩ በይፋዊ መስመሮቻችን እናሳውቃለን።"

> [!NOTE] Check the Feature Status page before using this example; if the feature has gone live, the answer changes.

### Information we cannot share
**EN:** "I understand you would like the seller's phone number to contact them directly. To protect the privacy of everyone on Byfluence, we cannot share personal contact details. I can pass your message to the seller through our team and let you know their response."

**AM:** "ሻጩን በቀጥታ ለማግኘት ስልክ ቁጥራቸውን እንደሚፈልጉ ይገባኛል። በByfluence ላይ ያሉትን ሁሉ ግላዊነት ለመጠበቅ የግል የመገናኛ መረጃ ማጋራት አንችልም። መልዕክትዎን በቡድናችን በኩል ለሻጩ አድርሼ ምላሻቸውን ላሳውቅዎት እችላለሁ።"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Put the answer in the first lines | Three paragraphs before the answer |
| Give a plain reason | "Due to policy" with no explanation |
| Offer real alternatives | Offer alternatives that aren't approved |
| Acknowledge disappointment | "That's just how it is." |
| "It will be reviewed" when policy is pending | "No" when nothing has been decided |

## Internal Notes
- Bad news is best delivered by the agent who knows the case. If a decision came from a department, ask them for the reason in plain words before you write to the customer.
- For high-value or sensitive refusals (e.g., seller ban), let Level 2 review the wording first.

# Customers threatening legal action, media or social posts
id: com-threats-legal-media
type: guide
category: difficult
audience: internal
tags: legal threat, lawyer, sue, court, media, social media post, ክስ, journalist, viral, threat
related: com-deescalation, pol-escalation-matrix, sop-escalation, pol-data-privacy, sop-security-incident, com-what-never-to-say
templates: tpl-acknowledge-complaint, tpl-escalation-notification, tpl-status-update
policy_pending: yes

## Summary
How to respond when a customer threatens to sue, go to the police or a regulator, contact journalists or post publicly: stay calm, keep helping, don't argue or admit liability, document exactly, and escalate to Management.

## Overview
Threats usually come from customers who feel unheard. Most can be resolved by taking the underlying problem seriously. But legal, regulatory and media matters can create real risk for Byfluence, so frontline agents never handle them alone and never make statements about legal responsibility.

## Guidelines
### For every threat
1. **Stay calm and respectful.** Do not react to the threat; respond to the problem.
2. **Acknowledge their right** to take whatever steps they choose, without challenge or encouragement.
3. **Keep helping** with the underlying issue through the normal process.
4. **Do not:** argue, threaten back, ask them not to post, admit fault or liability, discuss laws, say what Byfluence will or won't do legally, or offer compensation to stop the threat.
5. **Document** the exact words, channel, date and time in an internal note.
6. **Escalate** to the Supervisor, who informs **Management (Level 4)**. Priority at least High.

### Lawyers, police, regulators, journalists
- If someone identifies as a lawyer, police officer, regulator or journalist: be polite, do not confirm whether a person is a customer, do not share any information, and take their name, organization and official contact details. Pass them to Management.

### Social media posts
- Do not respond publicly from any account (official or personal) unless Management instructs it.
- Do not contact the customer to ask them to delete a post. Focus on resolving their case.
- Tell the Supervisor if you see a public post about Byfluence that names customers or includes private data.

### Threats of violence or self-harm
- Threats against staff, sellers or others: Trust & Safety and Management immediately, Critical.
- If a customer expresses intent to harm themselves, respond with care, encourage them to contact local emergency services or someone they trust, and inform the Supervisor at once.

## Examples
### "I will sue Byfluence!"
**EN:** "I understand that you are very unhappy with how this has been handled, {{customer_name}}, and you are free to take any steps you feel are right. I would still like to help resolve the issue itself. I have passed your case to a senior member of our team, who will review it. Your case number is {{ticket_id}}."

**AM:** "{{customer_name}}፣ ጉዳዩ በተያዘበት መንገድ በጣም ቅር እንደተሰኙ ይገባኛል፤ ተገቢ ነው ብለው የሚያስቡትን ማንኛውንም እርምጃ የመውሰድ መብት አለዎት። ቢሆንም ዋናውን ችግር ለመፍታት መርዳት እፈልጋለሁ። ጉዳይዎን ለቡድናችን ከፍተኛ ኃላፊ አስተላልፌዋለሁ፤ ይገመግሙታል። የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው።"

### "I'm going to post this everywhere."
**EN:** "I'm sorry your experience has made you feel this way, {{customer_name}}. My priority is to solve your problem. Here is what I am doing now: [action]. I will update you by {{follow_up_time}}."

**AM:** "{{customer_name}}፣ ያጋጠመዎት ሁኔታ ይህን ያህል ስላስቀየመዎት አዝናለሁ። የእኔ ቅድሚያ ችግርዎን መፍታት ነው። አሁን እያደረግሁ ያለሁት ይህ ነው፦ [እርምጃ]። እስከ {{follow_up_time}} ድረስ መረጃ እሰጥዎታለሁ።"

### Caller says they are a journalist
**EN:** "Thank you for calling. I'm not able to discuss any customer matters. If you share your name, organization and contact details, I will pass them to the appropriate team."

**AM:** "ስለደወሉ እናመሰግናለን። ስለ ማንኛውም ደንበኛ ጉዳይ መወያየት አልችልም። ስምዎን፣ ድርጅትዎን እና የመገናኛ መረጃዎን ቢሰጡኝ ለሚመለከተው ቡድን አስተላልፋለሁ።"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Respond to the problem, not the threat | "Go ahead, sue us." |
| Document exact words | Paraphrase loosely or skip logging |
| Escalate to Supervisor → Management | Handle legal or media contacts alone |
| Keep the case moving | Freeze the case because a threat was made |
| Neutral, factual language | Admit liability ("this is our fault, we'll pay") |

## Policy Pending Approval
- Approve the official contacts for legal, regulatory and media enquiries and the handoff process.
- Approve the social media response policy (who may post, approved wording).
- Approve the procedure for threats of violence and self-harm, including any local resources support may share.

## Internal Notes
- A legal threat does not change the facts of the case. Keep investigating and following the process.
- Never promise a customer compensation in exchange for not posting — it is unauthorized and can be seen as an admission.

# Supporting vulnerable or low-digital-literacy customers

## Summary
How to recognize and support customers who may need extra help — first-time internet users, older customers, people with disabilities, people in distress or financial hardship — with patience, simple steps and extra protection from scams.

## Overview
Byfluence's mission is that anyone can start and grow an online business. That includes people who have never paid online, never used Google Meet, or read slowly. These customers are also the most frequent targets of scams. Good support for them is simple, patient, and protective — and it never treats them as less capable.

## Guidelines
### Recognizing the need
Signs a customer may need extra support: very short or unclear messages, voice notes instead of text, repeated confusion about the same step, mentions of age, illness, disability or urgent financial need, a relative "helping", or high distress.

### How to help
- **Slow down.** One step per message or per sentence on the phone. Wait for confirmation before the next step.
- **Use simple words** and describe what they will see: "a green button that says Book Now at the bottom of the page".
- **Offer the channel that suits them**: a phone call for those who struggle with text; written steps for those who want to re-read.
- **Check understanding** gently: "Could you tell me what you see on your screen now?" — not "Do you understand?"
- **Repeat key information** (case number, next step) and offer to send it in writing.
- **Allow time**; never rush. Raise priority one level if they are distressed or something time-sensitive is involved.
- **Be patient with repeats.** Repeating is part of the help.

### Protection
- Be extra alert for scams: someone else asking on their behalf, sudden requests to change contact or payout details, payments they don't understand. Escalate concerns to Trust & Safety.
- **Relatives or helpers:** they may help the customer use the phone or app, but account-specific information is shared only with the account holder, who must be present and agree. Verification rules still apply.
- Remind them clearly and kindly that Byfluence never asks for codes, PINs or passwords.
- Never encourage purchases or upsells; explain options neutrally.

### Respect
- Do not talk down, over-simplify in a childish way, or comment on their abilities.
- Do not assume age, disability or literacy from a name or voice; respond to what you observe.

## Examples
### Guiding step by step
**EN:** "Let's do this together, one step at a time. First, please open the Byfluence website on your phone. Tell me when you see the home page, and I will tell you the next step."

**AM:** "አብረን፣ አንድ በአንድ እናድርገው። በመጀመሪያ እባክዎ በስልክዎ የByfluence ድረ-ገጽን ይክፈቱ። ዋናው ገጽ ሲታይዎት ይንገሩኝ፤ ቀጣዩን ደረጃ እነግርዎታለሁ።"

### A relative is helping
**EN:** "Thank you for helping. To protect the account, I need to speak with the account holder and confirm a few details with them. Could you please hand the phone to them? You can stay with them and help while we talk."

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| One step at a time | Send a list of ten steps |
| Describe what they will see | Use terms like "browser cache", "URL", "dashboard tab" |
| Offer a phone call | Insist on email only |
| Verify the account holder, even with helpers present | Share details with a relative "because they're family" |
| Warn kindly about scams | Lecture or blame them for sharing a code |
| Stay patient through repeats | Show impatience ("as I said") |
