## Summary
How to write natural, respectful Amharic for Byfluence customers: the እርስዎ form, common support phrases, how to mix English technical terms, and how to write numbers, dates and times without confusion between the Ethiopian and international calendars and clocks.

## Overview
Amharic is Byfluence's primary customer language. A message in natural, respectful Amharic tells the customer that Byfluence is an Ethiopian company that respects them. A stiff, machine-translated or informal message does the opposite. The biggest practical risk is **time and date confusion**: the Ethiopian clock and calendar differ from the international ones, and a misunderstanding can turn into a "no-show" dispute.

## Guidelines
### 1. Always use the respectful form
- Pronoun: **እርስዎ** (respectful "you"), never **አንተ** (informal, male) or **አንቺ** (informal, female). The respectful form is also gender-neutral, so you never need to guess the customer's gender.
- Possessives with **-ዎ**: ስምዎ (your name), አካውንትዎ (your account), ክፍያዎ (your payment), ጥያቄዎ (your request), ጉዳይዎ (your case).
- Requests with respectful verb forms and **እባክዎ**:

| Meaning | Respectful (use) | Informal (avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| Please send | እባክዎ ይላኩልኝ | ላክልኝ / ላኪልኝ |
| Please wait | እባክዎ ይጠብቁ | ጠብቅ / ጠብቂ |
| Please try | እባክዎ ይሞክሩ | ሞክር / ሞክሪ |
| Please check | እባክዎ ያረጋግጡ | አረጋግጥ / አረጋግጪ |
| Please log in | እባክዎ ይግቡ | ግባ / ግቢ |
| Please tell me | እባክዎ ይንገሩኝ | ንገረኝ / ንገሪኝ |

### 2. Names and titles
- Ethiopian names are usually **given name + father's name**; there is no family surname. Address customers by their **given name** (the first name): "ሰላም ዳዊት", not by the father's name.
- **አቶ** (Mr.), **ወ/ሮ** (Mrs.), **ወ/ሪት** (Miss) are polite, but use them only when you are sure. When unsure, use the given name alone — it is polite with the respectful verb forms. Never guess ወ/ሮ vs ወ/ሪት.
- Write the name exactly as the customer wrote it (Amharic or Latin script).

### 3. Common support phrases

| Purpose | Amharic | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting | ሰላም {{customer_name}}፣ / ጤና ይስጥልኝ | Hello / Good day (formal) |
| Welcome | እንኳን ወደ Byfluence ድጋፍ በደህና መጡ። | Welcome to Byfluence Support |
| Introducing yourself | ስሜ {{support_agent}} ይባላል። | My name is … |
| Offer help | እንዴት ልረዳዎት እችላለሁ? | How may I help you? |
| Thanks for contacting | ስላገኙን እናመሰግናለን። | Thank you for contacting us |
| Apology | ለተፈጠረው ችግር ይቅርታ እንጠይቃለን። | We apologize for the problem |
| Understanding | ሁኔታውን ተረድቻለሁ። | I understand the situation |
| Checking | ጉዳዩን እያጣራሁ ነው። | I am checking the matter |
| Wait a moment | እባክዎ ትንሽ ይጠብቁኝ። | Please wait a moment |
| Case number | የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው። | Your case number is … |
| Will update | አዲስ መረጃ እንዳገኘሁ አሳውቅዎታለሁ። | I will inform you when I have news |
| Security reminder | የይለፍ ቃልዎን፣ ፒንዎን ወይም የማረጋገጫ ኮድ ለማንም አያጋሩ። | Don't share your password, PIN or code with anyone |
| Anything else | ሌላ ልረዳዎት የምችለው ነገር አለ? | Anything else I can help with? |
| Closing | መልካም ቀን ይሁንልዎ። | Have a good day |
| Signature | ከሰላምታ ጋር፣ {{support_agent}}፣ Byfluence ድጋፍ | Best regards, …, Byfluence Support |

### 4. Mixing English terms
- Keep in **Latin script**: Byfluence, Google Meet, Chapa, Telebirr, M-Pesa, Telegram, placeholders (`{{ticket_id}}`), IDs and transaction references, and button labels exactly as they appear in the app/website.
- Use a common Amharic word and add the English term in brackets **the first time** if customers might know it only in English: "የግብይት መለያ ቁጥር (transaction reference)", "የይለፍ ቃል (password)", "የማረጋገጫ ኮድ (verification code / OTP)".
- Widely used loanwords are fine where they are clearer than a formal Amharic term: አካውንት (account), ሊንክ (link), ኤስኤምኤስ (SMS), ስክሪንሾት (screenshot), ኢሜይል (email).
- Button names: quote them as shown, e.g., "**Book Now** የሚለውን ይጫኑ" (press "Book Now").
- Say "ጉዳይ" (case) and "የጉዳይ ቁጥር" (case number) — not "ቲኬት".

### 5. Numbers and money
- Use ordinary digits (1, 2, 3) for amounts, IDs, dates and phone numbers — not Ge'ez numerals (፩፣ ፪፣ ፫).
- Money: "{{amount}} ብር". Do not add currency symbols the customer might not recognize.
- Read references and IDs back carefully; in Amharic writing, keep long numbers on their own line so they can be copied.

### 6. Dates — Ethiopian and international calendars
- The Ethiopian calendar has **13 months**: መስከረም, ጥቅምት, ኅዳር, ታኅሣሥ, ጥር, የካቲት, መጋቢት, ሚያዝያ, ግንቦት, ሰኔ, ሐምሌ, ነሐሴ, and ጳጉሜ (5 or 6 days). The Ethiopian year is 7–8 years behind the international (Gregorian) year; the Ethiopian new year (መስከረም 1) falls in September.
- Example: 18 November 2026 (international) = ኅዳር 9 ቀን 2019 ዓ.ም. (Ethiopian).
- **Rule:** always state which calendar you use. Write the date exactly as the Byfluence system shows it (with the international calendar marked **እ.ኤ.አ.**), and, if helpful for the customer, add the Ethiopian date marked **ዓ.ም.** Never convert in your head — use a converter you trust and double-check.
- Prefer writing the month in words, never ambiguous formats like 03/04.

### 7. Time — Ethiopian and international clocks
- The Ethiopian clock starts the day at sunrise: **international 7:00 AM = ከጠዋቱ 1 ሰዓት** in Ethiopian time. Ethiopian hour ≈ international hour minus 6.

| International | Ethiopian (spoken) |
|---|---|
| 7:00 AM | ከጠዋቱ 1 ሰዓት |
| 9:00 AM | ከጠዋቱ 3 ሰዓት |
| 12:00 noon | ከቀኑ 6 ሰዓት |
| 3:00 PM | ከቀኑ 9 ሰዓት |
| 6:00 PM | ከምሽቱ 12 ሰዓት |
| 8:00 PM | ከምሽቱ 2 ሰዓት |
| 12:00 midnight | ከሌሊቱ 6 ሰዓት |

- **Rule:** in any message about a session time, write the time as the system shows it, and add the Ethiopian-clock time in words in brackets: "3:00 PM (ከቀኑ 9 ሰዓት)". Also state the time zone for customers who may be outside Ethiopia (the `{{booking_time}}` placeholder should include it).
- When a customer gives you a time ("at 3"), ask which clock they mean before acting: "በኢትዮጵያ አቆጣጠር ነው ወይስ በውጭ አቆጣጠር?"

### 8. Punctuation and style
- Use Amharic punctuation: **።** (full stop), **፣** (comma), **፤** (semicolon), **፦** (before lists/quotes). A question mark "?" is normal.
- Keep sentences short. Avoid archaic or overly literary words; write as a respectful educated person speaks.
- Never paste raw machine translation. If you use a draft from a tool, rewrite it in your own natural Amharic and check that no customer data went into an unapproved tool.

## Examples
### Session reminder with both clocks
**EN:** "Hello Hirut, your consultation with {{seller_name}} is on {{booking_date}} at {{booking_time}}. Please join a few minutes early using the Google Meet link in your booking. Your case number is {{ticket_id}}."

**AM:** "ሰላም ሂሩት፣ ከ{{seller_name}} ጋር ያለዎት ምክክር {{booking_date}} በ{{booking_time}} ነው (ለምሳሌ 3:00 PM = ከቀኑ 9 ሰዓት በኢትዮጵያ አቆጣጠር)። እባክዎ በቀጠሮዎ ውስጥ ያለውን የGoogle Meet ሊንክ ተጠቅመው ጥቂት ደቂቃዎች ቀደም ብለው ይግቡ። የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው።"

> [!NOTE] In a real message, write the actual Ethiopian-clock time in brackets, not "for example".

### Clarifying a time
**EN:** "Just to be sure: when you say 3 o'clock, do you mean Ethiopian time (9 AM international) or international time (3 PM)?"

**AM:** "በትክክል ለመረዳት፦ 3 ሰዓት ሲሉ በኢትዮጵያ አቆጣጠር (ከጠዋቱ 3 ሰዓት፣ ማለትም 9:00 AM) ነው ወይስ በውጭ አቆጣጠር (3:00 PM፣ ማለትም ከቀኑ 9 ሰዓት)?"

### Informal vs respectful
**Avoid:** "ሰላም፣ የክፍያውን ደረሰኝ ላክልኝ።"
**Use:** "ሰላም {{customer_name}}፣ እባክዎ የክፍያውን ደረሰኝ ይላኩልኝ።"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| እርስዎ form throughout | Mix እርስዎ and አንተ in one message |
| Given name, or title only when sure | Guess ወ/ሮ / ወ/ሪት |
| "3:00 PM (ከቀኑ 9 ሰዓት)" | "at 3" with no clock stated |
| Mark dates እ.ኤ.አ. or ዓ.ም. | Unmarked dates like 03/04 |
| Latin script for Byfluence, Google Meet, IDs | Transliterate IDs or brand names into Ethiopic |
| Short sentences, Amharic punctuation | Long English-style sentences translated word for word |

## Summary
How to write English that Ethiopian customers — for most of whom English is a second or third language — understand easily: plain words, short sentences, no idioms, unambiguous dates and times, and an offer of Amharic.

## Overview
English is Byfluence's secondary support language, used when a customer writes in English or asks for it. Many English-writing customers are fluent; many others read English slowly or use translation tools. Diaspora customers and international buyers may also write in English and live in other time zones. Plain English serves all of them.

## Guidelines
- **Short sentences, common words.** "Please send the receipt" rather than "Kindly furnish the payment documentation at your earliest convenience".
- **No idioms or slang:** avoid "hang tight", "touch base", "ASAP", "no worries", "on our end", "FYI", "ping".
- **No unexplained abbreviations.** Write "transaction reference", not "txn ref".
- **Be polite but not over-formal.** "Please" and "thank you" are enough; avoid stacked formalities.
- **Dates:** write the month in words: "18 November 2026". Never "11/18/2026" or "18/11" — formats differ by country and calendar.
- **Times:** "3:00 PM East Africa Time (EAT)". For customers in Ethiopia, add the Ethiopian-clock time in brackets if they may read times that way. For customers abroad, include the time zone.
- **Money:** "{{amount}} ETB" or "{{amount}} Birr".
- **Offer Amharic** where natural: "If you prefer, we can continue in Amharic."
- **Keep the same structure** as Amharic messages: greeting, acknowledgement, numbered requests, case number, next step, signature.

## Examples
### Before and after
**Too complex:** "Kindly be advised that your transaction is currently pending reconciliation on our end; we'll circle back ASAP once we've touched base with the provider."

**Plain English:** "Hello Abel, your payment is still being checked with the payment provider. I will contact you again as soon as I have the result. Your case number is {{ticket_id}}."

**Amharic equivalent (if the customer prefers Amharic):** "ሰላም አቤል፣ ክፍያዎ አሁንም ከክፍያ አቅራቢው ጋር እየተጣራ ነው። ውጤቱን እንዳገኘሁ በድጋሚ አገኝዎታለሁ። የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው።"

### Offering Amharic
**EN:** "Thank you for your message. I'm happy to continue in English — or in Amharic, if you prefer."

**AM:** "ስለ መልዕክትዎ እናመሰግናለን። በእንግሊዝኛ መቀጠል እችላለሁ፤ ከፈለጉ ደግሞ በአማርኛ መቀጠል እንችላለን።"

### Session time for a diaspora customer
**EN:** "Your session is on {{booking_date}} at {{booking_time}}. Please check this against your local time before the session."

**AM:** "ክፍለ-ጊዜዎ {{booking_date}} በ{{booking_time}} ነው። እባክዎ ከክፍለ-ጊዜው በፊት ይህንን ሰዓት ካሉበት አካባቢ ሰዓት ጋር ያነጻጽሩ።"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Please send" | "Kindly furnish" |
| "18 November 2026" | "11/18/26" |
| "3:00 PM East Africa Time" | "3 o'clock" |
| "as soon as I have the result" | "ASAP" |
| Offer Amharic | Assume English is easy for everyone |

## Internal Notes
- If a customer's English reply suggests they did not understand, switch to simpler wording or Amharic — do not repeat the same sentence louder.
- Translation tools on the customer side may mangle idioms; plain English survives translation.

## Summary
How to communicate professionally on Telegram: pacing and length of messages, voice notes, stickers and emoji, formatting, editing messages, and the absolute rule of never using or sharing personal accounts.

## Overview
Telegram is likely to be the most-used support channel in Ethiopia. It feels personal and instant, which is an advantage — and a risk. Customers expect fast replies, may send voice notes and stickers, and can be targeted by fake "Byfluence support" accounts. Our Telegram communication must be friendly but professional, and every exchange must be recorded in the ticket.

## Guidelines
### Pacing
- **Acknowledge quickly.** If you need time to check, send a short message first: "Thank you, I'm checking this now." It stops the customer from sending ten more messages.
- **Don't flood.** Combine your answer into one or two messages rather than many single lines.
- **Mirror the customer's pace** when they are confused: one step per message, wait for their reply.
- **Don't leave a chat hanging.** If you must step away, say so and tell them when you will continue (only if you will).

### Length and format
- Short paragraphs, numbered steps. Telegram supports **bold** — use it sparingly for key items (case number, what to send).
- Keep each message readable on a small phone screen without scrolling.
- Do not send long legal or policy texts; summarize and link to official pages if available.

### Voice notes
- Customers may send voice notes — accept them, listen in private (headphones; never on speaker in a shared space), and summarize them in the communication log.
- **Reply in text** so there is a written record, unless the customer clearly cannot read text and your team allows voice replies.
- If a voice note is unclear, ask politely for the key details in writing (e.g., the transaction reference).

### Stickers and emoji
- Don't send stickers. Customers may send them — that's fine; respond to the content.
- Emoji: avoid in complaints, payment issues and security cases. In friendly how-to chats, at most one simple emoji (e.g., 🙏 for thanks) is acceptable if your team allows it.

### Editing and deleting
- If you made a mistake in a message the customer has already read, **send a correction** rather than silently editing — and log it.
- Ask customers to delete messages where they sent passwords or codes; delete such messages on our side if possible.

### Personal accounts — never
- Use only the official Byfluence support account. Never write to customers from your personal Telegram, never share your personal username or number, never add customers to groups.
- Warn customers about impersonators: Byfluence support will never ask for codes, PINs, passwords or "verification fees".

## Examples
### Quick acknowledgement
**EN:** "Thank you, {{customer_name}}. I'm checking this now — I'll reply in a moment."

**AM:** "እናመሰግናለን {{customer_name}}። አሁን እያጣራሁ ነው፤ በቅርቡ እመልስልዎታለሁ።"

### Responding to a voice note
**EN:** "Thank you for your voice message. I understood that your consultation link is not working for tomorrow's session. To check it, please type the consultant's name and the session time here."

**AM:** "ስለ ድምፅ መልዕክትዎ እናመሰግናለን። ለነገው ክፍለ-ጊዜ የምክክር ሊንክዎ እንደማይሰራ ተረድቻለሁ። ለማጣራት እባክዎ የአማካሪውን ስም እና የክፍለ-ጊዜውን ሰዓት እዚህ በጽሑፍ ይላኩልኝ።"

### Impersonation warning
**EN:** "Please note: Byfluence Support only contacts you from this official account. We will never ask for your password, PIN or any code. If someone else contacts you claiming to be us, please do not reply and let us know."

**AM:** "እባክዎ ልብ ይበሉ፦ Byfluence ድጋፍ የሚያገኝዎት በዚህ ይፋዊ አካውንት ብቻ ነው። የይለፍ ቃልዎን፣ ፒንዎን ወይም ማንኛውንም ኮድ በፍጹም አንጠይቅዎትም። ሌላ ሰው እኛ ነን ብሎ ካገኝዎት እባክዎ አይመልሱለት፤ ለእኛም ያሳውቁን።"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge within moments, then investigate | Leave the customer on "seen" |
| One or two complete messages | Ten one-line messages |
| Reply to voice notes in text | Send long voice notes back |
| Send a correction if you erred | Silently edit a message already read |
| Official support account only | Personal Telegram, personal number, groups |
| Log every exchange in the ticket | Treat Telegram as "off the record" |

## Internal Notes

# Email structure and subject lines
id: com-email-writing
type: guide
category: channels
audience: internal

related: sop-email, com-writing-clear-messages, com-english-guidelines, com-amharic-guidelines, pol-communication-standards

policy_pending: yes

## Summary
How to structure support emails and write subject lines so customers can find, understand and act on them — with the case number in every subject.

## Overview
Email customers usually write longer, more formal messages and may wait longer between replies. Each email we send should be complete on its own: a customer opening it days later must understand what it is about, what we did and what they need to do.

## Guidelines
### Subject lines
- Always include the **case number** and a short topic: "Byfluence Support — case {{ticket_id}}: payment check".
- Keep the same subject for the whole case so the thread stays together. Do not change it mid-conversation.
- Keep it short and factual; no capital letters or exclamation marks, no "URGENT" unless it is a security warning.
- For Amharic emails, use an Amharic subject with the case number in digits.

### Body structure
1. **Greeting** — "Hello {{customer_name}}," / "ሰላም {{customer_name}}፣"
2. **Thanks / empathy** — one sentence.
3. **Summary of the issue** — one sentence in your words.
4. **What we did or found** — short paragraph.
5. **What we need** — numbered list (if anything).
6. **Next step** — who does what next, without unapproved timelines.
7. **Security line** — when login or payment is involved.
8. **Case number** — if not already stated.
9. **Signature** — first name, Byfluence Support.

### Style
- Paragraphs of two to four lines. Use numbered lists for steps or requests.
- Answer **every** question in the customer's email — reread their email before sending.
- Attachments: mention them in the body ("I have attached…"). Never attach other customers' data or internal documents.
- Do not include internal notes, department names or escalation levels.

## Examples
### Subject lines
| Purpose | EN | AM |
|---|---|---|
| First reply | Byfluence Support — case {{ticket_id}}: your payment question | Byfluence ድጋፍ — ጉዳይ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}}፦ ስለ ክፍያዎ ጥያቄ |
| Update | Update on your case {{ticket_id}} | ስለ ጉዳይ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} አዲስ መረጃ |
| Resolution | Your case {{ticket_id}} has been resolved | ጉዳይ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ተፈትቷል |
| Information needed | Information needed for case {{ticket_id}} | ለጉዳይ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} የሚያስፈልግ መረጃ |

### Full email
**EN:**
"Hello {{customer_name}},

Thank you for contacting Byfluence Support. I understand that your seller application still shows as pending.

I have checked your application, and it is currently being reviewed by our seller team. There is nothing missing from your side at the moment.

I will update you as soon as the review is complete. If you have any questions in the meantime, simply reply to this email.

Your case number is {{ticket_id}}.

Best regards,
{{support_agent}}
Byfluence Support"

**AM:**
"ሰላም {{customer_name}}፣

Byfluence ድጋፍን ስላገኙን እናመሰግናለን። የሻጭ ማመልከቻዎ አሁንም በመጠባበቅ ላይ እንደሚያሳይ ተረድቻለሁ።

ማመልከቻዎን አጣርቻለሁ፤ በአሁኑ ጊዜ በሻጮች ቡድናችን እየተገመገመ ነው። በአሁኑ ሰዓት ከእርስዎ በኩል የሚጎድል ነገር የለም።

ግምገማው እንደተጠናቀቀ ወዲያውኑ አሳውቅዎታለሁ። እስከዚያው ጥያቄ ካለዎት ለዚህ ኢሜይል መልስ ብቻ ይላኩ።

የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው።

ከሰላምታ ጋር፣
{{support_agent}}
Byfluence ድጋፍ"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Case number in every subject | "Re: Re: Fwd: problem" |
| Keep the same thread | Start a new thread for each reply |
| Answer every question asked | Reply only to the last line |
| Numbered requests | Long paragraphs with hidden questions |
| Reply to the registered email for account details | Send account details to an unverified address |

# Phone etiquette and call structure
id: com-phone-etiquette
type: guide
category: channels
audience: internal
features: support-phone
tags: phone etiquette, call structure, ስልክ, hold, call opening, call closing, speaking clearly, spelling numbers
related: sop-inbound-calls, sop-outbound-calls, com-deescalation, com-vulnerable-customers, com-amharic-guidelines
templates: tpl-phone-inbound-opening, tpl-phone-outbound-intro, tpl-verify-identity
policy_pending: no

## Summary
How to sound professional and helpful on the phone: the seven-part call structure, voice and pace, putting callers on hold, reading numbers clearly, and ending calls well.

## Overview
Customers who call are often the most worried or the least comfortable with written tools. On the phone they cannot see your face or re-read your words, so clarity, patience and a calm voice matter even more. Every call must also end up recorded in the ticket, because the app does not capture calls.

## Guidelines
### The seven-part call structure
1. **Open** — greeting, Byfluence Support, your name, offer to help.
2. **Listen** — let the caller explain fully; take notes; use short signals ("I see", "እሺ", "ገባኝ").
3. **Identify & verify** — name, registered phone/email; verify before account details.
4. **Summarize** — repeat the problem in one or two sentences and ask if that is correct.
5. **Solve or plan** — guide them step by step, or explain what you will check and how you will contact them.
6. **Confirm** — case number, next step, channel for follow-up, anything they need to send.
7. **Close** — "Anything else?", thank them, polite goodbye. Then log the call.

### Voice
- Speak slowly and clearly, especially numbers and names. Smile — it is audible.
- Use the respectful Amharic form; use English only if the caller prefers.
- Avoid silence: if you are checking something, say so ("I'm checking your booking now, one moment please").

### Hold
- Ask permission before putting someone on hold, say why, and come back regularly to update them. If it will take long, offer a callback instead.

### Numbers and spelling
- Read IDs and references digit by digit, in groups: "4-7-2, 9-1-5".
- Ask the caller to repeat important numbers back to you.
- Offer to send the case number by Telegram or email as well.

### Difficult calls
- Do not interrupt or raise your voice. Use de-escalation techniques.
- If the caller becomes abusive after a polite warning, you may end the call courteously, then inform the Supervisor and log it.

## Examples
### Opening
**EN:** "Good morning, Byfluence Support, this is Liya speaking. How may I help you?"

**AM:** "እንደምን አደሩ፣ Byfluence ድጋፍ ነው፣ ሊያ እባላለሁ። እንዴት ልረዳዎት እችላለሁ?"

### Asking to hold
**EN:** "May I put you on hold for a moment while I check your booking? I'll be right back."

**AM:** "ቀጠሮዎን እስካጣራ ድረስ ለጥቂት ጊዜ እንዲጠብቁኝ ይፈቅዳሉ? ወዲያውኑ እመለሳለሁ።"

### Summarizing
**EN:** "So, if I understood correctly: you paid for the session yesterday evening, and the booking still shows as pending. Is that right?"

**AM:** "በትክክል ከተረዳሁ፦ ትላንት ማታ ለክፍለ-ጊዜው ከፍለዋል፤ ቀጠሮው ግን አሁንም በመጠባበቅ ላይ ነው የሚያሳየው። ልክ ነው?"

### Closing
**EN:** "Your case number is {{ticket_id}}. I will check with our payments team and contact you on Telegram. Is there anything else I can help you with today? Thank you for calling Byfluence."

**AM:** "የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው። ከክፍያ ቡድናችን ጋር አጣርቼ በቴሌግራም አገኝዎታለሁ። ዛሬ ሌላ ልረዳዎት የምችለው ነገር አለ? Byfluenceን ስለደወሉ እናመሰግናለን።"

## Do and Don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Let the caller finish | Interrupt with questions |
| Explain silences ("I'm checking…") | Long unexplained silence |
| Ask before hold; come back | Leave callers on hold without updates |
| Read numbers digit by digit | Rush through IDs |
| Log the call immediately after | Rely on memory at the end of the shift |

## Internal Notes
- Greetings vary by time of day in Amharic: እንደምን አደሩ (morning), እንደምን ዋሉ (afternoon), እንደምን አመሹ (evening). "ጤና ይስጥልኝ" works at any time.
- If a caller seems confused by the process, offer to stay on the line while they try each step.
