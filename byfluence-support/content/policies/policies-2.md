# Refunds & cancellations policy

type: policy
category: financial

policy_pending: yes

## Summary
Byfluence has **not yet approved** refund and cancellation rules. This document lists what is confirmed, how support must handle every request meanwhile, and every decision management must make for each offering category.

## Purpose
Give support a single reference for refund and cancellation requests so that requests are handled consistently and nobody promises or approves something the business has not decided.

## Scope
Refund, cancellation and reschedule requests from buyers and sellers for all offerings: one-on-one consultations, group consultations, and (when live) physical products, inventory-free/reseller products and digital products; plus payment errors such as duplicate charges.

## Confirmed Rules
- Refund eligibility, deadlines, deductions and approval authority **must be set by official policy and are not yet established**. Different categories may need different policies.
- The official workflow: complaint → identify transaction → collect evidence → review policy → contact seller/department → **authorized personnel decide** → explain to customer → process approved adjustments → document and close.
- Support never auto-approves refunds and never promises unconfirmed refund timelines.
- A refund can only concern a payment verified as Successful. Payment statuses include Refund Pending, Refunded and Disputed.
- A successful customer payment is not immediately withdrawable seller balance; completion, disputes, cancellations, refunds and settlement affect release.
- Byfluence is a marketplace facilitator; sellers are responsible for their offerings, subject to marketplace rules.
- Physical products, inventory-free selling and digital products are expansion areas — check the Feature Status page before discussing them as live.

## Rules for Support Staff
- Acknowledge every request, verify the customer and the payment, collect evidence, get the seller's side, and escalate to Senior Support / Supervisor (see *Handling refund requests*).
- Use neutral language: "Your request has been received and will be reviewed." Never "you will get a refund", "full refund", "partial refund", percentages, deadlines or processing times.
- Do not tell customers a refund is impossible either — say it will be reviewed, unless an authorized decision has been made.
- Record "Policy pending: [decision]" in the internal notes and include the case in the daily report's policy gaps.
- Payment errors (duplicate charges, failed-but-deducted) are handled as payment investigations by Payments & Finance, not as discretionary refunds.

## Policy Pending Approval
### General (all categories)
- Decide who may approve refunds (role) and any amount thresholds requiring Management.
- Decide whether platform fees/commissions are refunded, and whether payment-provider fees are deducted.
- Decide refund method (original payment method only? alternatives if the original method cannot receive refunds?).
- Approve customer-facing wording for refund processing times per payment method.
- Decide whether credits/vouchers may be offered instead of money, and under what conditions.
- Decide how seller earnings are adjusted when a refund is approved: before release (pending balance), after release (available balance), and after the seller has withdrawn.
- Decide how chargebacks/provider disputes are handled and communicated.
- Decide the maximum period after purchase/session in which a refund can be requested.
- Decide what evidence is required from buyers and sellers.
- Decide rules for repeated or abusive refund requests.
- Decide the appeal process when a buyer or seller disagrees with a decision.

### One-on-one consultations
- Buyer cancels: before the session, close to the session (define the cut-off), after the session start.
- Buyer requests reschedule: allowed? how many times? notice required? seller approval required?
- Seller cancels or reschedules: buyer options (refund vs. rebook), consequences for the seller.
- Seller no-show; buyer no-show; lateness of either party (see *Consultation attendance, lateness & no-show policy*).
- Session shorter than booked or ended early.
- Technical failure (missing/incorrect Google Meet link, connection problems) — who is responsible and what the buyer gets.
- Quality complaints ("not what was described", "unprofessional") — whether these can lead to refunds and how assessed.
- Payment succeeded but slot taken by another buyer.

### Group consultations
- Buyer cancels before the session; cut-off rules.
- Seller cancels the session or changes the date/time.
- Session full / capacity issues after payment.
- Buyer missed the session; partial attendance.
- Seller no-show or session ended early.
- Technical failure affecting some or all participants.

### Physical products (when live)
- Non-delivery, late delivery, lost shipments.
- Damaged, defective, wrong or incomplete items.
- Change-of-mind returns: allowed? period? condition of items?
- Who pays return delivery; who inspects returned items.
- Refunds of delivery fees.
- Responsibilities of seller vs. Byfluence vs. delivery partner.

### Inventory-free selling / reseller products (when live)
- Who is responsible for refunds: supplier, reseller, fulfillment partner or Byfluence.
- How the reseller's margin/commission is affected by refunds and returns.
- Returns logistics.

### Digital products (when live)
- Refunds after the product was accessed or downloaded.
- Broken links, corrupted or wrong files.
- Description mismatch.
- Copyright complaints and removed products.
- Download limits and access problems.

### Payment errors
- Approved process and wording for duplicate charges and failed-but-deducted payments (reversal vs. refund).

## Internal Notes
- Until this policy is approved, every refund request goes to Senior Support / Supervisor; Management decides exceptions. Record each decision and its reasoning — these precedents will shape the final policy.
- Customers often ask "what is your refund policy?" Answer honestly: requests are reviewed individually according to Byfluence's policy, and you will submit theirs for review. Do not improvise a rule.

## Summary
Rules for lateness, no-shows, technical failures and reschedules in one-on-one and group consultations are **pending approval**. This document sets out the confirmed facts, how support handles these cases now, and the decisions needed.

## Purpose
Attendance problems are the most common consultation dispute. Clear, fair rules protect buyers who paid and sellers who reserved their time.

## Scope
One-on-one and group consultations conducted via Google Meet, for buyers and sellers.

## Confirmed Rules
- The booking is confirmed after successful payment; the buyer receives Google Meet details; participants join at the scheduled time; the consultation is then marked completed, after which seller earnings become available per settlement rules.
- The process must handle missing links, incorrect times, time-zone confusion, late buyers/sellers, no-shows, connection problems and reschedule requests.
- **A scheduled meeting does not prove the service was delivered.**
- Group consultation rules for capacity, cancellation, rescheduling, refunds, missed sessions and seller no-shows are not yet approved.

## Rules for Support Staff
- Treat attendance complaints as disputes: get both sides and evidence before anything is decided (*Managing buyer–seller disputes*).
- Ask each party: when they tried to join (with time zone), what they saw (waiting room, "invalid link", nobody there), whether they contacted the other party and how, and any screenshots.
- Check the booking time and time zone in the system; time-zone confusion explains many "no-shows".
- If the session has not happened yet and one party cannot join (e.g., no link), treat as urgent: escalate to Technical for link problems and inform the other party where policy allows.
- Do not tell either party they "will be refunded", "will get a new session", or that the other side "broke the rules" before an authorized decision.
- Record attendance facts carefully; they may be used for seller conduct decisions.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Define the lateness grace period for buyers and for sellers, and what happens after it.
- Define a buyer no-show and its consequences (session forfeited, reschedule allowed, partial refund?).
- Define a seller no-show and its consequences for the buyer (refund/rebook) and for the seller (warning, effect on earnings, enforcement).
- Define what counts as evidence of attendance or absence (platform data, Meet information, screenshots, messages).
- Define responsibility when Google Meet or Byfluence systems fail.
- Define reschedule rules: who may request, notice period, number allowed, approval needed.
- Define how time zones are shown and which time zone is authoritative.
- Group consultations: rules when some participants cannot join, when the seller is late for everyone, and when a session is ended early.
- Define when a consultation may be marked completed if attendance is disputed.

## Internal Notes
- Until approved, all attendance disputes with money involved go to Senior Support / Supervisor; Management decides outcomes. Log each decision as a precedent.
- Many buyers try to join from a phone without the Google Meet app or with a different Google account; collect device details before concluding anyone was absent.

# Seller earnings release & payout policy

policy_pending: yes

## Summary
How seller earnings move from pending to available to withdrawn is confirmed at concept level; payout methods, minimums, timelines, fees and hold rules are **pending approval**.

## Purpose
Sellers depend on predictable payouts. Support must explain the earnings lifecycle accurately and route withdrawal problems correctly without promising dates or amounts.

## Scope
All seller questions and problems about earnings, balances and withdrawals.

## Confirmed Rules
- Lifecycle: customer pays → payment confirmed → recorded → platform fees calculated → seller earnings calculated → **pending** until release conditions are met → **available** (withdrawable) → seller requests withdrawal → Byfluence processes → recorded as completed after confirmation.
- Balance concepts: Total Earnings, Pending Balance, Available Balance, Withdrawn Amount, Withdrawal Pending.
- A successful customer payment does not mean immediately withdrawable balance (completion, disputes, cancellations, refunds and settlement apply).
- For consultations, earnings become available after the consultation is completed, per settlement rules.
- Payout methods, minimums and timelines are not confirmed. Commission rates are not confirmed.

## Rules for Support Staff
- Explain the lifecycle and balance terms; never quote commission rates, fees, minimums or payout dates.
- Verify the seller fully before discussing balances or withdrawals.
- Requests to change payout details are high-risk: full verification, then escalate to Payments & Finance; involve Trust & Safety if anything is suspicious.
- Withdrawal not received, wrong amount, balance looks wrong → collect the withdrawal date, amount, balance screenshots and escalate to Payments & Finance with the details (*Investigating payment issues*).
- Never tell a seller their earnings "will be released" or "are being held" unless Payments & Finance confirmed it.
- If a seller account may be compromised, follow *Handling a suspected security incident* immediately — withdrawals are a prime target.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Define release conditions per offering (consultations, group sessions, products, digital products) and any pending period.
- Approve payout methods (bank, mobile money, other), minimum withdrawal amount, withdrawal fees and processing times.
- Approve the commission/fee structure and how it is shown to sellers.
- Define holds: during disputes, refund investigations, suspected fraud, suspension or ban, and failed verification.
- Define the process and verification required to change payout details.
- Define what happens to earnings of suspended or banned sellers.
- Define tax/withholding obligations, if any (legal confirmation).
- Approve customer-facing wording for withdrawal processing times.

## Internal Notes
- Most "missing money" questions are about Pending vs Available balance. Explain the difference first, then check.
- Payout-detail change requests by email or Telegram from non-registered contacts are a classic fraud pattern.

policy_pending: yes

## Summary
The seller onboarding and verification steps and statuses are confirmed; required documents, review times, rejection reasons and re-application rules are **pending approval**.

## Purpose
Verified sellers build buyer trust and reduce fraud. Support must explain the process accurately and route verification problems to Seller Operations without making decisions.

## Scope
Seller applications, identity verification, verification statuses, rejections and questions about them.

## Confirmed Rules
- Onboarding: create account → apply to become a seller → provide personal/business information → submit identity verification → wait for review → approved or rejected → complete profile → create listings → sell after permissions are granted.
- Verification statuses: **Pending, Verified, Rejected**. Operational statuses: **Active, Suspended, Banned**. They are separate — a seller can be Verified but Suspended.
- Required documents and verification methods are not confirmed.
- Seller verification review is done by authorized staff (Seller Operations), not by Level 1 support and not by the AI assistant.

## Rules for Support Staff
- Explain the steps and statuses. Do not tell sellers which documents are required beyond what the application itself asks for, and do not promise review times or approval.
- Never accept identity documents through Telegram or email "to speed up verification" unless an approved process says so; direct sellers to the official application flow.
- Rejected sellers: share only the rejection reason recorded by Seller Operations, using their wording. If none is recorded, escalate to Seller Operations.
- Questions about a long-pending review → collect the application date and escalate to Seller Operations.
- Suspicious applications (documents appear edited, same documents on several accounts) → Trust & Safety.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Approve required documents for individuals and for businesses.
- Approve the verification methods (manual review, phone/email checks, others).
- Approve target review times and customer-facing wording.
- Approve the list of rejection reasons and the approved explanation for each.
- Decide re-application rules (waiting period, how many attempts).
- Decide additional requirements for regulated professions offering consultations (e.g., health, legal, financial advice).
- Decide retention and deletion of verification documents.

## Internal Notes
- The Feature Status page lists seller verification as part of MVP but the document list is unconfirmed — check it before answering detailed questions.

## Summary
Byfluence has not yet published an approved list of prohibited or restricted items and services. This document lists **proposals** for management, and how support handles listing reports meanwhile.

## Purpose
Keep illegal, dangerous, fraudulent and harmful offerings off the marketplace, and give sellers clear rules.

## Scope
All listings: consultations, group sessions and (when live) physical products, inventory-free/reseller products and digital products. Also reports from buyers about listings.

## Confirmed Rules
- Byfluence performs listing moderation and seller management (administration functions).
- Sellers are responsible for their offerings, subject to marketplace rules.
- No approved list of prohibited items exists yet.

## Rules for Support Staff
- When a buyer reports a listing (illegal, fake, misleading, offensive), thank them, create a ticket and escalate to **Seller Operations**; to **Trust & Safety** if it involves fraud, safety, exploitation or counterfeit goods. Priority High when there is potential harm.
- Do not tell the reporter what action will be taken against the seller; say it will be reviewed.
- When a seller asks why a listing was rejected, share only the reason recorded by Seller Operations. Do not tell sellers something is "allowed" or "not allowed" based on your own judgement or on the proposals below.
- Never remove a listing yourself unless your role permits it and Seller Operations confirmed (destructive action).

## Policy Pending Approval
Management to approve, amend or reject these **proposed** categories:
- **Proposed prohibited:** illegal goods or services under Ethiopian law; weapons, ammunition and explosives; illegal drugs and drug paraphernalia; stolen goods; counterfeit or replica branded goods; content or services that sexually exploit anyone or involve minors; sexual services; hate speech or services promoting violence; hacking, account selling, fake documents, fake reviews or followers; pyramid/Ponzi schemes and "guaranteed income" schemes; gambling services; sale of personal data; academic cheating services (exams taken for others).
- **Proposed restricted (allowed only with conditions or verification):** medical, legal, financial and psychological consultations (licensed professionals only?); pharmaceuticals and health supplements; alcohol and tobacco; cosmetics with health claims; electronics with SIM/telecom implications; used goods; educational courses claiming certificates or accreditation.
- Decide how "misleading claims" are defined (e.g., guaranteed results, earnings promises).
- Decide the process and timelines for listing review, removal, and seller notification.
- Decide whether buyers who reported a listing receive any outcome notice.
- Obtain legal confirmation of regulated categories in Ethiopia.

## Internal Notes
- The categories above are proposals only. Never quote them to sellers or buyers as rules.
- "Zero investment" claims by inventory-free resellers must not be presented as "zero cost or risk" — report such listings to Seller Operations.

# Seller conduct & enforcement (warnings, suspension, ban)

tags: seller conduct, warning, suspension, ban, seller suspended, appeal, የሻጭ ስነ-ምግባር, account suspended, enforcement
related: pol-prohibited-items, pol-consultation-attendance, pol-seller-verification, pol-seller-payouts, sop-buyer-seller-disputes
templates: tpl-acknowledge-complaint, tpl-escalation-notification, tpl-status-update
policy_pending: yes

## Summary
Seller operational statuses (Active, Suspended, Banned) are confirmed; the conduct standards, enforcement steps and appeals process are **pending approval**. Support routes complaints and status questions to Seller Operations and never decides enforcement.

## Purpose
Protect buyers and the marketplace's trust while treating sellers fairly and consistently.

## Scope
Complaints about sellers, questions from sellers about warnings/suspensions/bans, and support's role in documenting conduct issues.

## Confirmed Rules
- Operational statuses: **Active, Suspended, Banned**, separate from verification status (Pending, Verified, Rejected).
- Seller suspension is part of seller management, an administration function restricted by role.
- Sellers are responsible for their offerings, subject to marketplace rules.

## Rules for Support Staff
- Document every complaint about a seller factually in the ticket (what happened, evidence, booking/order IDs), even if the buyer does not want a refund. Patterns matter.
- Escalate conduct complaints to **Seller Operations**; harassment, threats, fraud or safety issues to **Trust & Safety** (High/Critical).
- Never warn, threaten, suspend or ban a seller yourself, and never tell a buyer what action will be taken.
- Suspended/banned sellers asking why: verify them, share only the reason and next steps recorded by Seller Operations; escalate if none recorded. Do not discuss earnings holds unless Payments & Finance confirmed.
- Treat sellers with the same respect as buyers; be neutral in all communication.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Approve the seller conduct standards (e.g., attendance and punctuality, accurate listings, respectful communication, no off-platform payment requests, no collecting buyer data, response expectations).
- Approve the enforcement ladder: which violations lead to a warning, temporary suspension, permanent ban; which lead directly to a ban (e.g., fraud).
- Decide how repeated no-shows, cancellations or poor reviews are counted.
- Decide the effect of suspension/ban on existing bookings, buyers already paid, and seller earnings.
- Decide the appeal process, who reviews appeals, and approved notification wording.
- Decide whether buyers are notified when a seller they booked is suspended.

## Internal Notes
- Sellers asking buyers to pay outside Byfluence (cash, direct transfer) remove the buyer's protection and Byfluence's revenue — always document and report to Seller Operations.
- Until approved, Seller Operations decides each case; support's job is complete, factual documentation.
