## Summary
A buyer asks how to buy a digital product (e-book, template, guide, course, design resource or document) on Byfluence and how they will receive it.

## Overview
> [!WARNING] **Planned feature.** Digital products are an expansion area and are **not confirmed as live**. Check the **Feature Status** page before telling a customer they can buy digital products. If it is not live, tell the customer it is not yet available and log their interest.

Confirmed flow: the seller uploads the digital product → the buyer pays → the buyer gets access. Access happens **through Byfluence** (e.g., a download button in the buyer's purchase history or order page), not by the seller or support sending files privately. Exact access rules — download limits, link expiry, streaming vs download for courses — are **not confirmed**.

Before buying, buyers should check:
- what exactly is included (number of pages, file format such as PDF/DOCX/PPTX/ZIP, language, course length);
- whether they have the software needed to open it (e.g., a PDF reader, Microsoft Word/Google Docs, Canva or design software);
- the seller's description and preview images.

## Customer Steps
1. Log in to your Byfluence account (purchases are linked to the account you use).
2. Open the digital product page and read the description, file format and what is included.
3. Click buy and complete payment using one of the methods shown at checkout.
4. Keep the receipt or SMS with the transaction reference.
5. After payment is confirmed, open your purchase history / orders page and use the download or access button.
6. Save the file to your phone or computer; do not share it with others (see the seller's usage terms).

## Summary
A buyer has bought a digital product and asks where and how to download or open it, or how to download it again on another device.

## Overview
> [!WARNING] **Planned feature.** Check the **Feature Status** page before treating digital products as live.

Purchased digital products are accessed **through the buyer's Byfluence account** — typically in purchase history / orders, with a download or access button. Exact screen names depend on the live system.

Common confusions:
- The buyer is logged into a different account than the one used to buy.
- On mobile, the file downloaded but the buyer cannot find it (it is in the phone's "Downloads" folder or Files app).
- The browser blocked the download or opened a PDF in a tab instead of saving it.
- Large files (courses, ZIP bundles) take longer on slow mobile data.

> [!WARNING] **Never send purchased files to customers manually through Telegram, email or any other channel** unless an approved procedure for that exists. Access must come through Byfluence so it is secure and recorded.

## Customer Steps
1. Log in with the same account you used to buy.
2. Open your purchase history / orders page and select the product.
3. Tap the download or access button.
4. **On a phone:** after downloading, open the "Files" or "Downloads" app, or the browser's downloads list.
5. **On a computer:** check the Downloads folder.
6. If the file opened in the browser instead of saving, use the browser's download/save option.
7. Use a stable internet connection (Wi-Fi if possible) for large files.

## Summary
The buyer paid for a digital product but has no download link or access button, or the product does not appear in their purchases.

## Issue Description
> [!WARNING] **Planned feature.** Check the **Feature Status** page to confirm digital products are live.

The buyer expected instant access after payment, so they are often anxious and suspect they have lost money. This is **High priority**. The cause is usually one of: payment not yet confirmed, access not granted because of a system error, the buyer looking in the wrong account, or a missing/removed file on the seller side.

## Possible Causes
- Payment still pending with the provider, so access has not been granted.
- Payment confirmed but the access/download record was not created (system error).
- The buyer is logged into a different account than the one used to buy.
- The seller removed or replaced the file, or the listing was taken down.
- The listing has no file uploaded (seller error).
- The confirmation message with the link went to spam or was never sent.

## Information to Collect
- Registered email/phone of the account used to buy
- Product name and seller name
- Date/time of payment, payment method and amount
- Transaction reference from the receipt/SMS
- Screenshot of the purchase history page

> [!WARNING] Never ask for passwords, OTP codes, PINs or full card numbers.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Verify the account holder.
2. Search the account for the purchase; also check other accounts with the same phone/email if the buyer has more than one.
3. Look up the transaction by reference:
   - **Payment pending/failed** → follow *pay-paid-order-not-confirmed*; tell the buyer not to pay again.
   - **Payment successful + purchase exists + no download** → check whether the listing still has a file. If yes → **Technical / Engineering**. If no file → **Seller Operations** to contact the seller.
   - **Payment successful + no purchase record** → **Payments & Finance** and **Technical / Engineering**.
   - **Paid twice** → *dig-duplicate-payment*.
4. Tell the buyer clearly that their payment is recorded (only if it is) and that access is being restored.
5. **Do not send the file yourself** through Telegram, email or any channel.
6. Record findings and references.

- [ ] Account confirmed
- [ ] Transaction checked
- [ ] Listing file checked
- [ ] Escalated to the right team

## Summary
The buyer can no longer download or open a digital product because a download limit was reached, the link expired, or access was restricted.

## Issue Description
> [!WARNING] **Planned feature.** Check the **Feature Status** page to confirm digital products are live. Download limits and expiry rules are **not confirmed** — never quote a number of downloads or an expiry period.

Download limits and expiring links protect sellers from unauthorized sharing. Buyers hit them when they try to download on a new phone, after losing the file, or after several failed downloads on slow connections. Access may also be restricted on purpose (suspected sharing, refund issued, chargeback, listing removed for copyright).

## Possible Causes
- The buyer used up the allowed downloads (often through failed/partial downloads).
- The access period ended.
- Access was revoked after a refund, chargeback or dispute.
- Access was restricted because of suspected sharing (many downloads from different locations).
- The listing was removed (e.g., copyright complaint) and access suspended.
- A system error showing a limit incorrectly.

## Information to Collect
- Account email/phone, product name, purchase date
- Exact error message (screenshot)
- How many times and on which devices they downloaded (their recollection)
- Why they need access again (new phone, lost file, failed downloads)

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Verify the account holder and the purchase.
2. Check the access record in the admin dashboard: download count, access status, any restriction reason.
3. Decide:
   - **Limit reached through failed downloads / new device** → request a reset from the authorized team (if a reset process exists).
   - **Access expired** → explain the access terms shown at purchase; route reset requests to Senior Support.
   - **Revoked after refund/chargeback** → explain politely that access ended with the refund.
   - **Restricted for suspected sharing** → do **not** reset; escalate to **Trust & Safety** (*dig-unauthorized-sharing*).
   - **Listing removed** → **Senior Support** (refund question) — do not disclose details of copyright cases.
   - **Looks like a system error** → **Technical / Engineering**.
4. Never send the file manually as a workaround.

## Summary
The buyer was charged more than once for the same digital product, or bought the same product twice by mistake.

## Issue Description
> [!WARNING] **Planned feature.** Check the **Feature Status** page to confirm digital products and the payment method involved are live.

Duplicate payments for digital products usually happen when access did not appear immediately and the buyer paid again, or when a payment page was submitted twice. Handle together with the general payment process in *pay-duplicate-charge*.

## Possible Causes
- Buyer paid again because the download link did not appear (see *dig-missing-download-link*).
- Payment page submitted twice (double tap, slow network).
- Buyer bought the same product from two accounts.
- A temporary hold that looks like a second charge but was reversed.

## Information to Collect
- Account email/phone, product name
- Transaction references for **each** payment, with date/time and amount
- Payment method
- Screenshots of the receipts/SMS (optional)

> [!WARNING] Never ask for PINs, OTP codes, passwords or full card numbers.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Verify the account holder.
2. Look up each transaction reference and check its status.
3. Decide:
   - **Two successful payments, one purchase** → escalate to **Payments & Finance** for the duplicate (follow *pay-duplicate-charge*).
   - **Two successful payments, two purchases** → buyer bought twice; record it and send to Payments for refund review of the extra purchase.
   - **Second payment pending/failed or reversed** → explain it is not a completed charge; advise checking with the provider.
4. Make sure the buyer has access to the product (one valid purchase).
5. Send *tpl-refund-request-ack* if a refund review is opened.

## Summary
A diagnostic playbook for any problem where a buyer cannot get, open or use a digital product they bought, routing each case to the right article and team.

## Overview
> [!WARNING] **Planned feature.** Digital products are planned. Check the **Feature Status** page first. If digital products are not live and a customer says they bought a "digital product", check whether they bought it outside Byfluence (e.g., directly from a seller's Telegram channel).

Golden rules:
1. Identify the account and purchase before anything else.
2. **Never send files manually** through Telegram, email or other channels unless an approved procedure exists.
3. Never quote download limits, expiry periods or refund rules until published.
4. Never ask for passwords, OTP codes, PINs or card numbers.
5. Ask for a screenshot early — it resolves most "can't find it" cases.

## Troubleshooting Steps
### Step 1 — Is there a confirmed purchase?
- **No purchase, payment taken** → *dig-missing-download-link* / *pay-paid-order-not-confirmed*.
- **No purchase, no payment** → the purchase did not happen; help them buy again (*dig-how-to-purchase*).
- **Two payments** → *dig-duplicate-payment*.
- **Wrong account** → ask them to log in with the account used to pay.

### Step 2 — Can they see the product in their purchases?
- **No** → *dig-missing-download-link* (High).
- **Yes, but no download button / error** → Step 3.
- **Yes, downloaded, can't find it** → *dig-download-access* (phone Files/Downloads).

### Step 3 — What does the error say?
| Message / symptom | Go to |
|---|---|
| Limit reached, expired, access denied | *dig-access-restricted-limit* |
| Download starts then fails | Check connection; retry on Wi-Fi; if repeated → **Technical / Engineering** |
| Generic error / page broken | **Technical / Engineering** with screenshot, device, browser |

### Step 4 — File downloaded but there is a problem
| Symptom | Go to |
|---|---|
| "Can't open", unknown format | *dig-file-wont-open* |
| Opens with errors, missing pages, broken ZIP, file size too small | *dig-corrupted-file* |
| Opens fine but it's a different product/file | *dig-incorrect-product* |
| Opens fine but content is poor / not as described | *dig-not-as-described* |
| Buyer wants money back | *dig-refunds* |

### Step 5 — Check for risk signals
- Buyer asks for the file to be sent to a different email/Telegram account.
- Repeated access-reset requests or many downloads from different places.
- Buyer says the product is someone else's work → *dig-copyright-complaint*.
- Seller reports buyers sharing/reselling → *dig-unauthorized-sharing*.
→ Add **Trust & Safety** where relevant.

### Step 6 — Route
| Situation | Department |
|---|---|
| Access not granted, download errors, platform bugs | **Technical / Engineering** |
| Payment, duplicate charges, refunds processing | **Payments & Finance** |
| Seller file missing/wrong/corrupt, seller unresponsive | **Seller Operations** |
| Access resets, refund recommendations, disputes | **Senior Support / Supervisor** |
| Copyright, sharing, fraud | **Trust & Safety** |

### Step 7 — Close properly
Confirm the buyer can open the correct file, or that a decision was made and communicated. Record root cause (payment pending, access error, wrong account, device/app, seller file, sharing) so management can set digital product rules.

## Escalation Conditions
- Multiple buyers of the same product report the same file problem → **Seller Operations** (seller file issue) and pause further escalations to Technical unless the platform is at fault.
- Multiple products failing to download → **Technical / Engineering** (possible incident); alert the supervisor.
- Any case where you consider sending the file manually → stop and ask **Senior Support / Supervisor**.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Confirm the access model (download, viewer, course player), limits and expiry.
- Approve the digital refund policy.
- Decide whether any manual delivery procedure exists and through which secure channel.
- Define seller obligations to fix or replace faulty files and the deadline for doing so.

## Internal Notes
- Most access tickets are solved by: right account + screenshot + correct app on the phone.
- Keep a list of products with repeated file complaints and share it with Seller Operations.
