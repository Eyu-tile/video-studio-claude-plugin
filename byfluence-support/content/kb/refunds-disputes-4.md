## Summary
What evidence to ask for in refund and dispute cases, how to ask for it simply, how to judge its quality, and what never to request.

## Overview
Evidence lets authorized personnel decide fairly. Ask only for what is relevant to the specific case — asking for everything frustrates customers and collects personal data we don't need.

### Evidence strength (strongest first)
1. **Byfluence system records** — booking/order status history, payment status, provider confirmation, access/download logs, message history on Byfluence.
2. **Payment provider records** — transaction references verified in the provider portal by authorized staff.
3. **Original, uncropped files from the customer** — photos with visible details, screenshots showing date/time and device clock.
4. **Cropped or forwarded screenshots** — useful but weak; can be edited.
5. **Statements without supporting evidence** — recorded, but marked unverified.

> [!NOTE] A scheduled Google Meet meeting does not prove the service was delivered. A "completed" status alone is not proof either. Look for evidence of what actually happened.

### What to ask for, by case type
| Case | Ask the buyer for | Ask the seller for |
|---|---|---|
| Consultation no-show | Screenshot of Meet waiting screen with device clock, time they joined, messages | Screenshot of their Meet screen at the time, messages, reason if absent |
| Session quality | What was promised vs. delivered (listing points), session duration | Session outline/materials, their account |
| Physical product damaged/wrong | Photos of item, damage, packaging, label; unboxing video if any | Pre-dispatch photos, dispatch/delivery record |
| Not delivered | Delivery address area, expected date | Dispatch/delivery proof |
| Digital product | Error screenshot, device, what part of content is wrong | Delivered file version, description |
| Payment | Transaction reference, receipt/SMS screenshot | — |

## Customer Steps
1. Take screenshots that show the whole screen, including the date and time if possible.
2. Take clear, well-lit photos of the item and packaging from several angles.
3. Keep original files; don't edit, crop or add text.
4. Send evidence only through official Byfluence support channels ({{support_channel}}).
5. Hide unrelated personal information (e.g., other bank transactions) if they wish — that is fine.

## Information to Collect
- Only the evidence relevant to the case type (table above)
- The date/time each file was created, if the customer knows
- Who took the photo/screenshot

> [!WARNING] Never request: passwords, OTP or verification codes, PINs, mobile-money PINs, full card numbers, CVV, full bank statements, ID documents (unless Trust & Safety specifically requests verification through the approved process), or screenshots of another person's private chat outside Byfluence.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Decide the case type and choose only the relevant items from the table.
2. Ask in simple, numbered steps (`tpl-request-screenshot`). For customers with low digital literacy, explain how to take a screenshot on their phone (e.g., press power + volume-down together on most Android phones) or offer to accept a photo of the screen taken with another phone.
3. When evidence arrives, attach the original file to the ticket; don't rename or compress it.
4. Assess each file: is the date visible? does it match the records? any signs of editing (mismatched fonts, misaligned numbers, cropped edges)?
5. Note the assessment in internal notes: "Evidence: 3 photos, clear, show crack on screen; packaging photo shows intact box" — factual only.
6. If evidence is weak, ask once more for a better version; if the customer cannot provide it, record that and escalate anyway.

## Escalation Conditions
- Evidence appears edited, reused or belongs to another account → **Trust & Safety**.
- Evidence includes another person's sensitive personal data (ID, medical, explicit content) → **Trust & Safety**; restrict access on the ticket.
- Customer cannot provide any evidence but the case must proceed → **L2** with a note.
- Evidence requires technical logs (Meet, downloads, notifications) → **Technical / Engineering**.

## Resolution Procedure
1. All relevant evidence attached to the ticket in original form, with a factual assessment note.
2. Missing evidence and the reason recorded.
3. Case forwarded to L2 or the investigating team.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Approve the official evidence standard for each offering type.
- Decide how long dispute evidence is retained and who can access it.
- Decide whether video evidence (e.g., unboxing) is required for high-value physical products.

## Internal Notes
- Replace every bracketed item before sending. Ask for at most three items at a time.
- Telegram compresses photos; if detail matters, ask the customer to send the photo "as a file" (document) in Telegram.
- Never forward evidence to the other party without L2 approval; blur personal data if L2 approves sharing.

## Summary
When and how to escalate refund and dispute cases, to which department, with what priority and what information, and how to keep the customer informed while it is escalated.

## Overview
Because refund policy is not yet approved, **every refund decision is an escalation** — front-line agents cannot decide any refund. Escalation is not a failure; it is the correct path. A good escalation is complete, routed to the right team, and prioritised correctly.

### Routing table
| Situation | Department | Suggested priority |
|---|---|---|
| Refund request ready for decision | Senior Support / Supervisor (L2) | Medium (High if session/order is time-sensitive) |
| Buyer–seller dispute | L2 | High |
| Payment not matching, duplicate, refund not processed, chargeback | Payments & Finance | High |
| Seller earnings/payout affected | Payments & Finance | Medium–High |
| Platform error (Meet link, booking time, downloads) | Technical / Engineering | High |
| Listing misleading, seller pattern | Seller Operations | Medium |
| Fraud signals, account takeover, refund to different account | Trust & Safety | High–Critical |
| Threats, harassment | Trust & Safety (Management if threats of violence) | High–Critical |
| High value, legal threats, media, regulator, police | Management (via L2) | Critical |

## Information to Collect
- The neutral case summary (`ref-dispute-investigation` format)
- All references: ticket, transaction, order/booking, both account IDs
- Evidence from both sides, attached
- What has been told to the customer so far, and any follow-up time already promised
- Why you are escalating and what decision or action you need

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Check that the case is complete enough to act on. If key information is missing and the customer can provide it, ask first — unless the case is urgent.
2. Choose the department and priority from the routing table. When in doubt between two, escalate to L2 and say which other team may be needed.
3. Write the escalation note:
   - **Ask**: "Decision needed on refund request" / "Please verify provider status" / "Please review possible fraud".
   - **Summary**: the neutral case summary.
   - **Deadline driver**: any time sensitivity (upcoming session, payout about to be released).
4. Send the customer an escalation notice (`tpl-escalation-notification`) with a follow-up time you can keep.
5. Keep ownership: you remain responsible for updating the customer unless the ticket is formally reassigned.
6. If the receiving team has not acted by your follow-up time, chase once, then inform your supervisor.

## Escalation Conditions
- See the routing table. Additionally:
- Any safety threat → `tns-threats-safety` immediately, before anything else.
- Customer explicitly requests a supervisor → **L2**.
- Customer contacts more than one channel about the same dispute → merge tickets, then escalate once.
- Escalation unanswered after your follow-up time → chase, then **supervisor**.

## Resolution Procedure
1. The receiving team acknowledges and records an owner.
2. The decision or action is recorded on the ticket.
3. You (or the new owner) communicate the outcome and close per `ref-final-decision-communication`.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Approve internal response targets for each department and priority.
- Approve value thresholds that require Management approval.
- Approve who owns customer communication after escalation (original agent vs. receiving team).

## Internal Notes
- Never escalate with "customer angry, please help". State the ask and the facts.
- Never tell the customer "the manager will refund you" or name an internal person; say "our specialised team".
- Only promise a follow-up time you control (when **you** will update them), not when the other team will decide.

## Summary
How to tell buyers and sellers the outcome of a refund request or dispute: clearly, neutrally, in their language, with the reason and the next step — using only what authorized personnel decided.

## Overview
The decision message is often the only part of the process the customer remembers. It must be:
- **Accurate** — matches the decision record exactly (outcome, amount, reason).
- **Clear** — states the outcome in the first lines; no jargon.
- **Neutral** — does not blame the other party or the review team.
- **Respectful** — acknowledges the customer's experience whatever the outcome.
- **Complete** — explains what happens next (processing, earnings release, or how to submit new evidence).
- **Parallel** — buyer and seller each receive the outcome relevant to them, without the other's private details.

### Possible outcomes
| Outcome | Buyer message | Seller message |
|---|---|---|
| Full refund | `ref-full-refund` | Transaction refunded; earnings for this transaction adjusted |
| Partial refund | `ref-partial-refund` | Partial refund; remaining earnings follow normal settlement |
| Rejected | `ref-refund-rejected` | Request not approved; earnings follow normal settlement |
| Alternative agreed (reschedule, replacement, fixed file) | Confirm the agreed alternative | Confirm what they must do and by when (per decision) |

## Information to Collect
- Decision record: outcome, amount, reason, decision maker, date
- Each party's language preference and channel
- Processing details from Payments (if money moves)

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Read the decision record. If anything is unclear (amount, reason, what the seller must do), ask L2 before writing.
2. Write the buyer message and the seller message separately. Use the outcome-specific article/template.
3. Put the outcome in the first two sentences. Then reason. Then next step.
4. Use Amharic by default; English if the customer prefers.
5. Send in writing. If you also call, follow the same script and then send the written version.
6. Record on the ticket: messages sent, channels, dates.
7. If money moves, set a follow-up to confirm processing (`ref-refund-status`).

## Summary
A buyer or seller does not accept a refund or dispute decision and wants it reviewed again. The agent acknowledges the disagreement, checks for new information, and routes it as an appeal without reopening the decision themselves.

## Issue Description
Disagreement is normal and the customer has a right to be heard respectfully. However, front-line agents cannot change or reverse decisions. A formal appeal process **has not been approved yet**, so all appeals go to **Senior Support / Supervisor (L2)** for a decision on whether a second review happens. The customer may also threaten a bank chargeback, legal action, or social-media posts; stay calm and professional.

## Possible Causes
- The customer has new evidence not seen in the first review
- The customer believes evidence was misread or the reason is wrong
- The decision reason was not explained clearly
- The customer feels unheard or that Byfluence favoured the other party
- The decision record contains an error (wrong amount, wrong transaction)

## Information to Collect
- Case number and the decision being appealed
- Specifically what the customer disagrees with
- Any **new** evidence or information
- The outcome the customer is asking for

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Listen and acknowledge: "I understand you disagree with this decision, and I want to make sure your concerns are reviewed."
2. Check for an obvious error in the record (wrong amount/transaction). If found, escalate to L2 as a correction, not an appeal.
3. Ask what they disagree with and whether they have new information.
4. Attach new evidence and a short appeal note to the original ticket (do not open a new dispute).
5. Escalate to **L2** marked "Appeal". If the customer asks for a manager, L2 decides whether Management is involved.
6. Tell the customer that the earlier decision stays in place until the review is complete.
7. If they mention a bank/provider chargeback, do not discourage or encourage it; inform **Payments & Finance**.
8. If they threaten public posts, stay neutral; inform L2. Never threaten the customer back.

## Escalation Conditions
- Every appeal → **Senior Support / Supervisor (L2)**.
- Error in the decision record → **L2** (correction).
- Customer requests Management, or threatens legal/regulatory action → **L2**, who decides on **Management**.
- Customer opened a chargeback → **Payments & Finance**.
- Customer becomes abusive or threatening → **Trust & Safety**.

## Resolution Procedure
1. L2 records whether a second review is granted and its outcome.
2. Communicate the outcome (`ref-final-decision-communication`). If upheld, say clearly that the decision is final under current procedures, if L2 confirms that.
3. Record: appeal reason, new evidence (yes/no), outcome, reviewer.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Decide whether a formal appeal process exists, who reviews appeals, and any appeal deadline.
- Decide how many reviews a case can receive before the decision is final.
- Decide the approved wording for "final decision".

## Internal Notes
- Do not say "there is nothing more I can do" — say what you **are** doing (passing it for review).
- Do not hint that the decision might change. Neutral: "it will be reviewed".
- If the customer repeats the same appeal without new information, L2 may decide the decision stands; log each contact but avoid re-escalating identical appeals repeatedly.

## Summary
A single decision playbook for any refund request or buyer–seller dispute, from the first message to closing the ticket, following the confirmed Byfluence support workflow.

## Overview
The confirmed workflow (from the Byfluence business overview):

**Complaint → Identify transaction → Collect evidence → Review policy → Contact seller/department → Authorized personnel decide → Explain to customer → Process approved adjustments → Document and close.**

Golden rules:
1. **You never approve or reject a refund.** Authorized personnel decide.
2. **No invented rules.** No deadlines, percentages, fees or processing times unless officially approved.
3. **Neutral between buyer and seller.** Both are customers.
4. **Safety and fraud first.** Threats, account takeover or fraud signals override the normal flow.
5. **Privacy.** Never share one party's personal data with the other.
6. **Never ask for passwords, OTP codes, PINs or card numbers.**

## Troubleshooting Steps
### Step 0 — Safety and fraud check (always first)
- Threats of violence or self-harm, or someone in danger → stop and follow `tns-threats-safety`.
- "I didn't make this payment" / account taken over → `tns-unauthorized-transaction` / `tns-account-compromise`.
- Refund requested to a different account → `ref-refund-to-different-account`.
- Seller asked buyer to pay off-platform → `tns-off-platform-payment-request`.
Otherwise continue.

### Step 1 — Complaint received
- Confirm identity (registered email/phone). Create one ticket; merge duplicates from other channels.
- Ask: what happened, what outcome do you want? Send acknowledgement with {{ticket_id}} (`tpl-refund-request-ack`).

### Step 2 — Identify the transaction
- Find order/booking and payment. Check payment status:
  - Pending / Failed / Canceled → not a refund yet; use payment articles.
  - Successful → continue.
  - Two successful payments → `pay-duplicate-charge`.
  - Refund Pending / Refunded → `ref-refund-status` or `ref-refund-delay`.
  - Disputed → `pay-chargeback`; link tickets.
- Identify the offering: consultation (`ref-consultation-refunds`), physical product (`ref-product-refunds`), digital product (`ref-digital-refunds`). For planned features, check the **Feature Status** page.

### Step 3 — Can it be fixed without a refund?
- Missing Meet link before session, broken download link, wrong file → fix first (Technical / Seller Operations).
- If the customer accepts the fix, record it and close after confirmation. If not, continue.

### Step 4 — Collect evidence
- Use `ref-evidence-collection`. Ask only for relevant items. Capture the listing and records now.

### Step 5 — Review policy
- Check Announcements for any newly approved refund policy. Until then: **Policy Pending → escalate**.

### Step 6 — Contact seller / department
- Seller's account of events (neutral request) — `ref-buyer-seller-disagreement` / `ref-seller-disputes-refund`.
- Payments for status/holds, Technical for platform errors, Logistics for deliveries.

### Step 7 — Escalate for decision
- Write the neutral case summary (`ref-dispute-investigation`) and route per `ref-dispute-escalation`.
- Tell the customer when **you** will update them.

### Step 8 — Explain the decision
- Use `ref-final-decision-communication` (and `ref-full-refund`, `ref-partial-refund`, `ref-refund-rejected`).
- Disagreement → `ref-customer-disagrees-decision`.

### Step 9 — Process approved adjustments
- Payments & Finance processes refunds; confirm status **Refund Pending → Refunded**.
- Seller earnings adjustments are handled by Payments.

### Step 10 — Document and close
- [ ] Outcome and reason recorded
- [ ] Decision maker recorded
- [ ] Amounts (paid / requested / approved / processed) recorded
- [ ] Root cause category recorded (seller no-show, buyer no-show, technical, damaged, not delivered, not as described, digital access, change of mind, fraud)
- [ ] Both parties informed
- [ ] Follow-up actions logged (listing fix, seller review, Trust & Safety)
