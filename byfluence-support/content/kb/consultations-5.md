## Summary
The buyer attended the consultation but is dissatisfied with its quality — it was unhelpful, generic, disorganised or unprofessional — and may ask for a refund.

## Issue Description
The session took place for its full time, but the buyer feels it was not worth the price. Quality complaints are subjective and hard to judge: Byfluence is a **marketplace facilitator** and the seller is responsible for the content of their consultation. Support's job is to listen, separate *subjective dissatisfaction* from *objective problems* (not as described, too short, misconduct), collect specifics, and route to the right process. Refund eligibility for quality complaints is **policy pending**.

### Triage: what kind of complaint is it?
| The buyer says… | It is probably… | Go to |
|---|---|---|
| "The topic was different from the listing" / "they're not qualified as claimed" | Not as described | `con-not-as-described` |
| "It was much shorter than booked" | Duration dispute | `con-duration-dispute` |
| "They were rude / insulting / inappropriate" | Conduct | `con-inappropriate-conduct-in-session` |
| "It was interrupted" | Incomplete session | `con-incomplete-consultation` |
| "It wasn't helpful / too general / I expected more" | Subjective quality | This article |

## Possible Causes
- Mismatch between buyer expectations and what the listing actually offered.
- The seller was unprepared, generic or poorly organised.
- Language barrier (e.g., session in English when the buyer expected Amharic).
- Buyer's question was outside the consultant's area.
- Poor audio/video made the session hard to follow.

## Information to Collect
- Booking ID, consultant, date/time
- What specifically was unsatisfactory (ask for concrete examples)
- What the buyer expected, and which part of the listing created that expectation (screenshot of listing if possible)
- Whether they raised it with the consultant during or after the session
- What outcome the buyer is asking for (feedback only, follow-up session, refund)

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Listen and acknowledge; let the buyer explain fully.
2. Triage with the table above — route objective problems to their articles.
3. For subjective quality: collect specifics and compare them with the listing text (topic, description, duration, language if stated).
4. Contact the seller neutrally for their account (`tpl-seller-contact-request`) — share the concern in summary, not the buyer's exact words if emotional.
5. If the buyer only wants to give feedback → record it and explain how reviews/ratings work if that feature is live (check Feature Status).
6. If the buyer asks for a refund or follow-up → escalate to **Senior Support / Supervisor** with the listing, the complaint details and the seller's response.
7. Record the complaint category for quality monitoring; repeated complaints about one seller → **Seller Operations**.

## Summary
The buyer says the consultation was materially different from what the listing promised — a different topic, a different person, a different format, or credentials that seem false.

## Issue Description
Unlike general dissatisfaction, "not as described" is about a **measurable gap** between the listing and the session: e.g., the listing says "business plan review" but the session was a sales pitch; a different person than the listed consultant attended; the listing says Amharic but the session was in English only; the listing claims a qualification the consultant admits they don't have. This can be a listing-accuracy problem (Seller Operations) and in serious cases misrepresentation or fraud (Trust & Safety).

## Possible Causes
- Listing text is vague or outdated.
- The seller changed the content/format without updating the listing.
- Someone other than the listed consultant delivered the session (account sharing).
- Exaggerated or false credentials.
- The session was used to sell another product or service.
- The buyer misunderstood the listing (check carefully).

## Information to Collect
- Booking ID, consultant, date/time
- The specific listing claims that were not met (screenshot of the listing **as it was when booked**, if the buyer has it)
- What actually happened in the session (specific examples)
- Whether the person in the session was the listed consultant
- Any messages or materials from the session

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Open the listing in the admin dashboard and check its current content and, if available, its edit history (was it changed after the booking?).
2. Compare the buyer's specifics with the listing's claims. Decide: material gap / misunderstanding / unclear.
3. Contact the seller for their account.
4. If a **different person** delivered the session, or credentials appear false → **Trust & Safety** (identity/misrepresentation) and **Seller Operations**.
5. If the session was used to push off-platform sales or payments → **Trust & Safety**.
6. Escalate the buyer's request (refund/other) to **Senior Support / Supervisor** with the comparison.
7. Flag the listing to **Seller Operations** for review if it is misleading, regardless of the refund outcome.

## Summary
A buyer or seller reports that the other party behaved inappropriately during a consultation — harassment, threats, sexual or discriminatory comments, intimidation, pressure for off-platform payment, or sharing explicit content.

## Issue Description
This is a **safety** issue first and a service issue second. The reporter may be shaken, embarrassed or afraid of retaliation. It can involve a seller toward a buyer, a buyer toward a seller, or a participant in a group session. Because a one-on-one session has only two people, the reported person will usually know who reported them — handle with care.

### Severity
| Level | Examples | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Threats of violence, sexual harassment, explicit content, extortion, anything involving a minor, ongoing contact outside the platform | Critical — Trust & Safety immediately, notify supervisor |
| **High** | Discriminatory or degrading remarks, intimidation, demands for off-platform payment, sharing the other person's personal data | High — Trust & Safety |
| **Medium** | Rudeness, unprofessional language, dismissive behaviour | Medium — Senior Support; Trust & Safety if repeated |

## Possible Causes
- Deliberate harassment or abuse.
- A disagreement that escalated.
- Cultural or language misunderstandings (still take it seriously and let Trust & Safety assess).
- Scam behaviour (pressure to pay outside Byfluence, request for personal/financial data).

## Information to Collect
- Booking ID, date/time, who was involved
- What happened, in the reporter's own words (do not push for graphic detail — ask what they are comfortable sharing)
- Evidence they already have: screenshots, chat messages, files, any recording **they already made** (do not ask them to record)
- Whether contact is continuing outside the platform (phone, Telegram, social media)
- Whether they feel unsafe now

> [!WARNING] Do not ask the reporter to contact, confront or "test" the other person. Do not ask for passwords or codes. If someone is in immediate danger, advise them to contact local emergency services or police first.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Thank them, believe the report enough to act, and assess severity using the table.
2. If there is immediate danger → advise local emergency services / police; notify a supervisor immediately.
3. Collect what they are comfortable sharing; preserve evidence in the ticket (attachments, timestamps).
4. Advise them on safety steps: stop communicating with the person, block them on personal apps if contact moved off-platform, do not share more personal information.
5. Escalate to **Trust & Safety** with severity, evidence and booking details. Do **not** contact the reported person yourself — Trust & Safety decides how and when.
6. If there are further bookings between the same people, flag them to Trust & Safety (they may need to be paused).
7. If the reporter also asks for a refund → note it; outcome is decided separately by authorized staff after Trust & Safety review.
8. Keep the reporter's identity and details confidential within the team.

## Summary
A buyer wants to record the consultation or receive a recording, notes or materials — or a seller asks whether they may record. Rules on recording and materials are not yet approved; consent is essential.

## Issue Description
Typical questions: "Can I record the session?", "Will I get a recording?", "The consultant promised slides — I haven't received them", "Can I record my group session to sell it later?". Recording involves the **privacy of everyone in the session**. Google Meet's built-in recording is only available on certain Google account types, and participants are notified when it starts; screen- or phone-recording tools can record without notice. Byfluence has **not** yet set rules on recording or materials.

## Possible Causes
- Buyer wants to review advice later.
- Listing mentioned materials (slides, templates, notes) that were not delivered.
- Seller wants to reuse recordings (e.g., for courses — digital products are a planned feature).
- One party discovered they were recorded without consent.

## Information to Collect
- Booking ID, consultant, date/time
- What exactly is requested (permission to record, a recording, materials)
- What the listing says about recordings/materials (screenshot)
- Whether both parties agreed (and how — in writing on the platform is best)
- If recorded without consent: when, how they know, what was recorded

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. **Permission to record:** explain that recording is only acceptable with the clear agreement of everyone in the session, and that the buyer should ask the consultant before the session starts. Byfluence does not provide recordings itself (unless confirmed otherwise on the Feature Status page).
2. **Requested recording not provided:** if the listing promised a recording → treat as `con-not-as-described`; contact the seller. If not promised → it is up to the seller.
3. **Promised materials not delivered:** check the listing; contact the seller (`tpl-seller-contact-request`) to deliver through an appropriate channel; if they refuse → `con-not-as-described`.
4. **Recorded without consent / recording shared publicly:** treat as a privacy matter → **Trust & Safety**.
5. **Group sessions:** every participant must agree; a seller wanting to reuse group recordings commercially needs explicit policy approval → **Senior Support / Supervisor**.

## Summary
The buyer asks whether they can get a refund for a consultation. The consultation refund policy is not yet approved — support records the request, collects evidence and escalates; never promise or refuse a refund.

## Issue Description
Buyers ask about refunds in many situations: before a session ("If I cancel, do I get my money back?"), after a problem (no-show, cancellation, short session, poor quality), or as a general question before booking. Because **refund eligibility, deadlines, deductions and approval authority are not established**, agents must not give a yes/no answer. Instead: explain the process, record the request with its timing and reason, and route it.

### Situation → route
| Situation | Article |
|---|---|
| Buyer wants to cancel a future session | `con-cancellation-by-buyer` |
| Seller didn't join | `con-seller-no-show` |
| Seller cancelled | `con-cancellation-by-seller` |
| Session too short / interrupted | `con-duration-dispute` / `con-incomplete-consultation` |
| Not as described / poor quality | `con-not-as-described` / `con-quality-complaint` |
| Charged twice / paid but not booked | payments articles |
| General question before booking | This article |

## Possible Causes
- Change of plans; problem with the session; payment error; general pre-purchase question.

## Information to Collect
- Booking ID or consultant + date/time
- Reason for the refund request (and which situation above it is)
- Payment method and transaction reference
- Evidence relevant to the situation (see the situation article)
- Time of request (recorded on the ticket)

> [!WARNING] Never ask for card numbers, PINs, passwords or verification codes to "process a refund". Refunds are processed by Payments & Finance using the original transaction reference.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Identify the situation and open the matching article.
2. Collect the information and evidence needed.
3. Explain the process neutrally: request recorded → reviewed under Byfluence's policies → outcome communicated.
4. Escalate to **Senior Support / Supervisor**; approved refunds are processed by **Payments & Finance** (see `ref-consultation-refunds`, `ref-how-to-request-refund`).
5. For pre-booking questions: say that refund rules for consultations are being finalised and that any request is reviewed case by case; suggest reading the listing carefully and checking the time before paying.

## Summary
Explains what "completed" means for a consultation, how it relates to seller earnings, and what to do when a booking is completed (or not) incorrectly. The exact completion mechanism is not yet confirmed.

## Overview
In the confirmed flow, after the session takes place the consultation is **marked completed**, and then seller earnings for that booking move from **pending** to **available** according to settlement rules (`out-pending-vs-available`).

What is **not confirmed** yet:
- Whether completion is automatic (after the scheduled end time), done by the seller, confirmed by the buyer, or a combination.
- Whether a buyer can dispute completion, and for how long.
- How long after completion earnings become available.

Common questions:
| Who asks | Question | Answer approach |
|---|---|---|
| Seller | "The session happened — why is it not completed?" | Check status; explain completion triggers when confirmed; escalate if stuck. |
| Seller | "When can I withdraw?" | Earnings follow settlement rules; no timelines unless approved. |
| Buyer | "It says completed but the session never happened!" | Do not treat "completed" as proof of delivery → `con-seller-no-show`. |
| Buyer | "Do I need to confirm anything?" | Depends on the completion mechanism — check Feature Status. |

> [!WARNING] A booking marked "completed" — like a scheduled meeting — **does not prove** the session was delivered. If a buyer disputes it, follow the no-show/incomplete process and tell Payments a dispute is open.

## Customer Steps
1. **Sellers:** after the session, check the booking status in your seller dashboard; if a "mark complete" action exists, use it only after the session actually took place.
2. **Buyers:** check the booking in your dashboard; if it is marked completed but the session did not happen or was incomplete, contact support as soon as possible.

## Escalation Conditions
- Session took place but booking is stuck as not completed → **Technical / Engineering** (status bug) or **Payments & Finance** (settlement).
- Buyer disputes a "completed" status → **Senior Support / Supervisor** via `con-tsg-no-show-decision-guide`; inform **Payments & Finance** of the dispute.
- Seller asks why available earnings are delayed → payouts articles / **Payments & Finance**.
- Seller marks sessions complete that did not happen → **Trust & Safety**.

## Internal Notes
- Never change booking statuses yourself unless an approved procedure allows it.
- Never give sellers payout timelines that are not approved.
- If a seller marks bookings complete right after the start time, repeatedly, check for no-show complaints — possible abuse.
