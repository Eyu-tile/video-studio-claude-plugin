## Summary
Explains to buyers and sellers how to report abusive behavior, scams, fake listings or policy violations to Byfluence, what to include, and what happens after they report — including how their identity is protected.

## Overview
Any user can report another user or listing. Reports are reviewed by **Trust & Safety**. Whether Byfluence has an in-app "Report" button on profiles, listings or messages is **not confirmed** — until it is, reports come through official support channels ({{support_channel}}) and are logged as tickets.

**What can be reported**
- Harassment, insults, threats or discrimination
- Fraud or scams, including requests to pay outside Byfluence
- Fake, counterfeit or misleading listings
- Someone pretending to be Byfluence staff
- Misuse of personal information
- Any other breach of marketplace rules

**What happens after a report**
1. The report is logged with a case number.
2. Trust & Safety reviews it (and may ask the reporter for more information).
3. Action may be taken on the reported account or listing.
4. The reporter is told the review is complete — but, for privacy, **not** the details of any action against another user.

**Identity protection**: the reported user is not told who made the report.

## Customer Steps
1. If you are in immediate danger, contact local police or emergency services first.
2. Stop replying to the person; block them where you can.
3. Take screenshots of the messages, profile or listing (include the date/time if possible).
4. Contact Byfluence Support through an official channel ({{support_channel}}) and say you want to make a report.
5. Share: who (name/profile link), what happened, when, where (Byfluence messages, Google Meet, phone, Telegram), and your screenshots.
6. Keep your case number. Never share your password, PIN or verification codes with anyone — Byfluence will never ask.

## Summary
A buyer or seller believes someone else has accessed or taken over their Byfluence account — unknown logins, changed email/phone/password, bookings or orders they didn't make, messages they didn't send, or changed payout details. Act immediately to limit damage and escalate to Trust & Safety as Critical.

## Issue Description
An account takeover can lead to stolen money (seller withdrawals, refunds redirected), purchases on the victim's payment method, scams sent to the victim's contacts, and exposure of personal data. **Seller accounts with an available balance are the highest risk.** The customer may still be able to log in, or may already be locked out because the attacker changed the email/phone and password.

Recovery itself (restoring access, verifying identity) is a Trust & Safety / account team action. The front-line agent's role: help the customer secure what they can, stop money movement through escalation, and collect the facts.

## Possible Causes
- The customer shared an OTP/verification code with someone pretending to be Byfluence or a buyer
- Password reused from another hacked site, or easy to guess
- Phishing link to a fake login page
- Phone or SIM stolen, or someone close has access to the phone
- Malware on a shared or public computer
- Session left logged in on a shared device

## Information to Collect
- Customer name and the registered email/phone **as they remember it** (if changed by the attacker, both old and new if known)
- Can they still log in?
- What they noticed: unknown bookings/orders, changed details, messages sent, withdrawal requests, password reset emails they didn't request
- When they last logged in normally
- Whether they shared any code or clicked a link (ask yes/no; never ask for the code)
- For sellers: available balance and whether payout details were changed or a withdrawal requested
- Other ways to confirm identity that Trust & Safety accepts (do not invent your own checks)

> [!WARNING] Never ask for passwords, OTP codes, PINs or card numbers — even "to check". Never change account email/phone or payout details on request through chat. Be alert: the person contacting you could be the attacker.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. **If they can still log in** → ask them to change their password now (to one not used elsewhere), and log out of other devices if that option exists.
2. **If locked out** → do not attempt recovery yourself unless your role has an approved recovery process; collect facts and escalate.
3. **Stop money movement**: for sellers, immediately ask **Payments & Finance** to hold pending withdrawals and check payout details; for buyers, check for new bookings/orders/refund requests.
4. **Check account activity** (through authorized tools): recent changes to email/phone/password, payout details, new devices/locations, bookings/orders/messages.
5. **Escalate to Trust & Safety** as **Critical** with: what changed, when, money at risk, and how the customer contacted you (registered or new contact?).
6. Send `tpl-account-compromise-urgent` to the customer.
7. If the customer's payment method was used, follow `tns-unauthorized-transaction` as well.
8. If the attacker sent scam messages to other users, tell Trust & Safety so they can warn affected users.
9. Give security advice: unique password, never share codes, check the email account linked to Byfluence is also secure.

- [ ] Customer told to change password (if possible)
- [ ] Payments asked to hold withdrawals (sellers)
- [ ] Account changes reviewed
- [ ] Escalated to Trust & Safety — Critical
- [ ] No changes made on chat request

## Escalation Conditions
- Every suspected compromise → **Trust & Safety**, **Critical**.
- Seller with balance, changed payout details or recent withdrawal request → **Payments & Finance** immediately (hold) + Trust & Safety.
- Unknown payments on the customer's payment method → `tns-unauthorized-transaction` (Payments + Trust & Safety).
- Many accounts compromised at once, or login system issue suspected → **Technical / Engineering** + **Management** (`tns-security-incident-report`).

## Resolution Procedure
1. Trust & Safety confirms the account is secured and the owner verified.
2. Payments confirms the status of any held or completed money movement.
3. Unauthorized bookings/orders/messages handled (cancelled, refunded, other users warned) by the owning teams.
4. Customer informed via verified contact details; security advice given.
5. Record cause (if known), what changed, money at risk, and actions.

## Policy Pending Approval
- Approve the identity verification process for account recovery (what proves ownership).
- Decide Byfluence's responsibility for losses from compromised accounts.
- Decide whether automatic holds apply when account contact or payout details change.
- Confirm whether two-step verification/OTP exists and how it is reset.

## Internal Notes
- Attackers often contact support pretending to be the victim, to "recover" the account to their own email. Never change contact details on chat requests.
- A request to change payout details or refund destination in the same week as a password/email change is a red flag.
- Keep the tone calm: victims are often embarrassed about having shared a code. Don't blame them.

## Summary
Someone reports a security vulnerability in Byfluence (e.g., they can see other users' data), or a possible security incident (data leak, mass account takeovers, suspicious system behavior). Record precisely, do not test or confirm anything, and escalate to Trust & Safety, Technical and Management.

## Issue Description
Reports may come from customers who accidentally saw someone else's booking or data, from security researchers, or from several customers reporting the same strange behavior at once. Some reporters ask for a reward or threaten to publish the issue. The agent must take the report seriously, avoid confirming or denying anything about Byfluence's systems, and get it to the right people fast. Whether Byfluence offers any reward for vulnerability reports is **not decided** — never promise one.

## Possible Causes
- A bug that shows one user's data (bookings, orders, messages, documents) to another user
- Payment or booking manipulation (e.g., changing price in a request)
- Exposed files, links or documents (e.g., verification documents reachable without login)
- Phishing campaigns or fake websites (see `tns-impersonation-of-byfluence`)
- Mass account compromise suggesting a credential leak
- A researcher testing Byfluence without permission

## Information to Collect
- Reporter's name and contact (they may want to stay anonymous — record what they give)
- What they observed, step by step, and when
- The page/URL or feature involved
- Screenshots — ask them to **blur other people's personal data** before sending
- Whether they accessed, downloaded or shared any data that is not theirs
- Whether they have told anyone else or plan to publish

> [!WARNING] Never ask the reporter to "try again" or demonstrate the issue further. Never ask for passwords, OTP codes or PINs. Do not try to reproduce the issue yourself on live customer data.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Thank the reporter. Ask them not to access or share any more data and to keep the details confidential while Byfluence investigates.
2. Collect the information above in one message; don't ask them to perform further tests.
3. Do **not** confirm the vulnerability, discuss impact, or guess the cause.
4. Escalate immediately to **Trust & Safety**, **Technical / Engineering** and **Management** (priority High; **Critical** if customer data or money is actively exposed).
5. If the reporter asks for a reward, a deadline, or threatens publication → note it exactly and escalate to Management; do not negotiate.
6. If multiple customers report the same unusual behavior (e.g., seeing others' bookings), alert your supervisor — this may be a live incident.
7. Use only approved wording for any customer-wide communication; Management decides.

## Summary
Concerns that a seller verified with fake, altered or someone else's identity documents — or a person reports that their identity (ID card, name, photo) was used without permission to create a Byfluence account. Route to Trust & Safety and Seller Operations; never share or discuss verification documents.

## Issue Description
Sellers complete identity verification before selling (statuses **Pending, Verified, Rejected**). The exact documents required are **not yet confirmed**. Identity fraud undermines trust in the whole marketplace. Reports may come from:
- A person whose ID was stolen and used to open a seller account
- A buyer who believes a seller is not who they claim to be
- An agent or reviewer who notices a document that looks altered
- A seller who suspects a competitor is using a fake identity

Verification documents are highly sensitive personal data. Only authorized reviewers handle them.

## Possible Causes
- Stolen or lost ID card used by someone else
- Edited or forged document image
- Real person's photo/name copied from social media
- A family member or acquaintance using someone's documents
- A genuine seller whose public name differs from their legal name (not necessarily fraud)

## Information to Collect
**From a person reporting identity theft**
- Their name and contact details
- The Byfluence account/profile they believe uses their identity (link or name)
- How they found out
- Whether they reported the lost/stolen ID to the authorities (they decide; do not require it)
- Do **not** ask them to send their ID through chat — Trust & Safety will request any proof through a secure, approved process.
**From a buyer or other reporter**
- The seller profile and what seems wrong

> [!WARNING] Never ask for passwords, OTP codes or PINs. Never send, show or describe anyone's verification documents to anyone. Never confirm to a reporter what documents a seller submitted or whether they are verified beyond what the public profile shows.

## Troubleshooting Steps
1. Acknowledge and thank the reporter; explain that identity concerns are handled by a specialised team.
2. Collect the information above. Capture the public profile as it is now.
3. Escalate to **Trust & Safety** (identity fraud) and **Seller Operations** (verification review), priority **High**; **Critical** if the account is actively selling or has a balance to withdraw.
4. If the reported account has pending withdrawals, ask **Payments & Finance** to review holds (their decision).
5. Do not contact the reported seller yourself.
6. If an agent/reviewer noticed a suspicious document internally, write an internal escalation only — never mention it to the seller.

## Issue Description
Customers trust Byfluence with names, contact details, addresses (for deliveries), payment references and, for sellers, identity documents. Privacy complaints include: another user sharing or misusing their contact details or photos; receiving messages they didn't consent to; a data exposure they believe came from Byfluence; or a staff member mishandling information. - A bug exposed data to other users (see `tns-security-incident-report`)
- A support conversation was handled in a way that exposed information
- Customer believes Byfluence sold or shared their data

3. Collect evidence; restrict ticket visibility if it contains sensitive data.
4. Escalate to **Trust & Safety** (priority High); Management for system exposure, staff mishandling or legal threats.
5. Do not speculate about how the data leaked.
