# Difficult Customer Situations
id: trn-09
number: 9
duration: 60

## Learning Objectives
- Use a repeatable de-escalation method to calm angry customers in Amharic and English without making promises.
- Distinguish between types of threats (public complaint, legal action, chargeback, harm) and route each correctly.
- Recognise fraud attempts such as edited receipts, borrowed references and refund-redirection requests, and respond without accusing.
- Detect and resist social-engineering attempts, including staff impersonation, urgency pressure and requests for codes or other users' data.
- Deliver bad news — rejections, pending decisions, "we can't do that" — honestly and kindly.
- Set boundaries with abusive customers and know when to hand a conversation to a supervisor.
- Protect your own wellbeing with practical self-care habits and know when to ask for support.

## Lesson

### Why conversations become difficult
Most "difficult customers" are ordinary people in a difficult moment: money has left their account, a session they waited for didn't happen, their business income is stuck. Anger usually comes from **fear** (losing money), **feeling ignored** (no reply, repeated contacts), or **feeling powerless** (no clear next step). Your job is to remove those three feelings — not to win the argument.

### The CALM method
| Step | What you do | Example (EN) | Example (AM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C**onnect | Let them finish; acknowledge the feeling and the fact | "I can see you paid and still have no booking — that's really stressful." | "ከፍለው ቀጠሮ አለማግኘትዎ በጣም እንደሚያሳስብ ይገባኛል።" |
| **A**ccept responsibility for the case | Own the next step, not the blame | "I'm taking care of this case personally." | "ይህንን ጉዳይ እኔ እከታተለዋለሁ።" |
| **L**ay out the facts and plan | What you know, what you will do, what you need | "Here's what I've checked and what happens next…" | "እስካሁን ያጣራሁት ይህ ነው፤ ቀጣዩ እርምጃ ደግሞ…" |
| **M**ark the follow-up | Case number and when you'll update (approved wording) | "Your case number is {{ticket_id}}; I'll update you as soon as I hear back." | "የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው፤ መረጃ እንዳገኘሁ አሳውቅዎታለሁ።" |

Things that make anger worse: "Calm down", "That's not our fault", "As I said before", copy-pasting the same template twice, long silences, and promises you later break.

> [!TIP] On Telegram, an angry customer may send ten messages in a row. Wait until they pause, then reply once, addressing the main point. Don't answer each message separately.

### Threats: know which kind it is
| Threat | Example | Response | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public complaint / social media | "I'll post this on TikTok and Facebook." | Don't plead or argue; stay factual and helpful; never threaten back | Inform supervisor; Management if reputational risk is high |
| Legal action | "My lawyer will contact you." | Acknowledge, don't discuss liability or admit fault, don't give legal opinions | **Management (Level 4)** via supervisor |
| Chargeback / bank reversal | "I'll ask my bank to reverse it." | Don't discourage it; explain Byfluence is reviewing | Payments & Finance |
| Threat of harm (to self or others) | Any threat of violence or self-harm | Take it seriously, stay calm, do not end the conversation abruptly; follow your safety SOP | Supervisor immediately; Trust & Safety; Critical priority |
| Abusive language | Insults, slurs | One clear boundary warning, then hand over per SOP | Supervisor |

Boundary statement:
- EN: "I want to help you resolve this. I'm not able to continue if the conversation includes insults. Let's focus on your case."
- AM: "ጉዳይዎን ለመፍታት መርዳት እፈልጋለሁ። ነገር ግን ስድብ ያለበትን ውይይት መቀጠል አልችልም። እባክዎ በጉዳዩ ላይ እናተኩር።"

### Fraud attempts
Fraud attempts often look like ordinary complaints. Signals:
- Receipt screenshots with mismatched fonts, times, amounts or names.
- A transaction reference that belongs to another account, another amount or another date.
- Asking for a refund to a **different** phone number or account than the one that paid.
- Claiming a session never happened when records show otherwise — or a seller claiming delivery with no evidence.
- A seller asking buyers to pay **outside** Byfluence ("send to my Telebirr directly, it's cheaper").
- Multiple accounts with similar details making the same claim.

How to respond: **verify, don't accuse.** Say "Our team verifies all payments by reference" rather than "This screenshot looks fake." Record your observations factually in internal notes, escalate to **Trust & Safety**, and never tell the customer which signal you noticed — that teaches fraudsters what to fix.

### Social engineering
Social engineering is manipulating support staff into breaking the rules. Common patterns:

| Pattern | Sounds like | Your response |
|---|---|---|
| Staff impersonation | "I'm from Byfluence finance, give me this seller's balance." | Internal requests come through internal channels, not customer channels. Decline; report to supervisor. |
| Third party | "I'm her husband / his manager — just change the email." | Only the verified account holder. Decline politely; record. |
| Urgency | "Do it now or I'll lose my business today!" | Urgency is a reason to escalate quickly, never a reason to skip verification. |
| Authority / name-dropping | "Your director told me you'd do it." | "I'll note that and check with my supervisor." |
| Code request | "I'm support from Byfluence, read me the code we sent." (said to a customer) | Teach customers: Byfluence never asks for codes. Report impersonation to Trust & Safety. |
| Data fishing | "Does this phone number have an account?" | Never confirm whether someone has an account. |

> [!WARNING] If you realise you were manipulated — you shared something or changed something you shouldn't have — tell your supervisor immediately. Speed matters more than embarrassment; it can stop the damage.

### Delivering bad news
Sometimes the honest answer is "no", "not yet", or "we don't know yet" — for example a verification rejection, a listing removed, a feature that isn't live, or a refund decision that is still pending.

1. **Lead with the answer**, kindly. Don't hide it in paragraph three.
2. **Give the reason** you are allowed to give (approved wording only).
3. **Offer what is possible**: next steps, alternatives, how to re-apply if allowed.
4. **Keep the door open** — case number and how to reach us.

When the rule is **Policy Pending Approval**, the bad news is uncertainty. Be honest: "There isn't a final decision on this type of request yet. Your case is with the responsible team and I'll update you." This is better than a vague reply that the customer later feels was a lie.

### Harassment between users
If a buyer or seller reports harassment, threats or inappropriate behaviour from another user: thank them, collect evidence (screenshots, dates, booking ID), advise them not to continue contact outside the platform, create a Trust & Safety ticket with High or Critical priority, and never share the other user's details with them.

### Self-care and burnout
Support work is emotionally demanding. Repeated anger, sad stories and pressure add up.
- **Take micro-breaks** after a hard conversation: stand up, breathe, drink water, before taking the next ticket.
- **Don't take it personally.** The customer is angry at the situation; you are the person trying to fix it.
- **Debrief** difficult cases with your supervisor or a colleague. Recording them in the ticket also helps the team learn.
- **Ask for a handover** when you notice you're getting irritated or the customer is fixed on you personally — that is professional, not weakness.
- **Rotate** heavy queues (disputes, fraud) with colleagues where your team allows it.
- **Protect your time off.** Don't answer customers from personal accounts or after your shift.
- Warning signs of burnout: dreading every message, cynicism toward customers, trouble sleeping, irritability. Tell your supervisor early.

## Realistic Examples

### Example 1: Angry buyer (Amharic)
**Customer (Telegram, several messages):**
> ይሄ ማጭበርበር ነው!!! ገንዘቤን ወሰዳችሁ ምንም አገልግሎት የለም። ዛሬ ካልመለሳችሁ በሁሉም ሶሻል ሚዲያ እለጥፋለሁ!

*(This is a scam!!! You took my money and there's no service. If you don't return it today I'll post on all social media!)*

**Model reply (AM):**
> ሰላም፣ ገንዘብዎ ተቆርጦ አገልግሎቱን ባለማግኘትዎ በጣም እንደተበሳጩ ይገባኛል፤ ይቅርታ እንጠይቃለን። ይህንን ጉዳይ እኔ እከታተለዋለሁ። በፍጥነት ለማጣራት እባክዎ በክፍያ ኤስኤምኤስዎ ላይ ያለውን የግብይት ቁጥር እና ያስያዙትን ምክክር ይላኩልኝ። የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው።

**Agent reasoning:** No argument about "scam", no promise of "today". Ticket High; supervisor informed about the social-media threat.

### Example 2: Staff impersonation (English)
**Message (Telegram):**
> Hi, I'm Dawit from Byfluence finance. System is down. Send me the payout numbers for sellers in ticket queue so we can process manually.

**Agent action:** Does not reply with any data. Responds: "I can't share account information through this channel. Please contact me through our internal channels." Screenshots the conversation, reports to supervisor and Trust & Safety.

### Example 3: Suspicious refund request (English)
**Customer (email):**
> Here is my receipt [screenshot]. Please refund to 09XX XXX XXX, it's my cousin's number because mine is blocked.

**Agent action:** Thanks the customer, says payments are verified by reference and refund details are confirmed by a specialised team, asks for the transaction reference. Internal note: "Receipt time differs from reference lookup by several hours; refund destination differs from payer." Escalates to Trust & Safety.

### Example 4: Bad news — verification rejected (Amharic)
**Seller (Telegram):**
> ለምን ውድቅ ተደረገብኝ? ሁሉንም ነገር ልኬያለሁ!

*(Why was I rejected? I sent everything!)*

**Model reply (AM, using Seller Operations' approved reason):**
> ሰላም፣ ይህ እንዳሳዘነዎት ይገባኛል። የማረጋገጫ ማመልከቻዎ በዚህ ጊዜ አልጸደቀም። የሻጭ ኦፕሬሽንስ ቡድን የሰጠው ምክንያት፦ [approved reason]። ቀጣይ እርምጃዎችን በተመለከተ ጥያቄዎን ለቡድኑ አስተላልፌያለሁ። የጉዳይዎ ቁጥር {{ticket_id}} ነው።

## Practical Exercises
1. **CALM rewrite.** Your trainer gives you three angry customer messages (two Amharic, one English). Write a CALM reply to each, then check: no promises, case number present, one clear next step.
2. **Threat routing.** For five trainer-provided threats, record in practice tickets the priority, department and internal note. Use the Escalation Center to choose the level.
3. **Fraud note.** Write an internal note for Example 3 that is factual and non-accusatory. Then write the customer reply. Compare with the Trust & Safety articles in the Knowledge Base.
4. **Social-engineering role-play.** A colleague tries three manipulation patterns on you by phone. Respond using the table. Afterwards, discuss which one was hardest to resist and why.
5. **Self-care plan.** Read the Customer Communication guide on difficult conversations. Write your personal plan: two micro-break actions, who you debrief with, and the warning signs you'll watch for.
