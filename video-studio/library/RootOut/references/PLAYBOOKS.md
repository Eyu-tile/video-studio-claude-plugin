- framingType: provenance-first
- **unique mechanism:** No product-level differentiator is established in the saved research: standard 500 mg moringa leaf capsules. Avatar Fragmentation is the marketing pattern, not an ingredient mechanism. The differentiation described here is the creative system.

- **creative style:** 
- **core moves:** 1. Map every possible avatar for the ingredient
Before building the brand, list 15+ distinct avatars with specific pain points your ingredient could address. Don't skip avatars because they seem 'small' — the long tail IS the play. Each avatar gets its own ad with its own hook, symptom, and emotional register. · 2. One avatar, one ad, one symptom
Every ad speaks to exactly ONE avatar. Never 'this is great for everyone.' Always 'if you have X.' Instantly dismissible by non-targets = scale for targets. Make the ads cheaply and quickly; quantity beats polish. · 3. Ancient-provenance credibility + viral + low price
Use '5,000 years of traditional use' as the proof leg. Layer scarcity ('selling out,' 'we restocked today'). Keep price under $25 to remove purchase friction. This combo enables ad-buying at volume.

## TheraPet

- **hero product:** Two separate plug-in calming diffuser products: Dog Calming Diffuser and Cat Calming Diffuser. This review contains nine dog ads and one cat ad; evidence is labelled by product.
- **pattern:** Symptom Ladder
- **main angle:** Dogs: skepticism overturned; three-month result; older couple avoiding rehoming; morning cleanup/furniture costs; emotional-wellbeing mistake; visiting Mom again. Cats: relief from litter-box accidents through a cat-specific safety-signal story. Shared outcome-first montage is supported, but one cat ad cannot establish its recurrence across the cat library.
- **hook structure:** Outcome-first testimony; direct symptom question; disbelief opener; mistake reframe; relationship stakes. A repeated body with a new opening is a variation, not a new DNA pattern. See the ten product-labelled ads for exact scripts.
- **unique mechanism:** Symptom Ladder
- **proof type:** stars: 4.9 / 5
- reviewCount: 75512
- authorityBadge: Used in 2,000+ rescue shelters
- **creative style:** 
- **core moves:** 1. Find the lived-experience language
Stop using the product's clinical terms. Interview 5 real customers and capture the EXACT words they use to describe their daily suffering. Those words become your ad copy. 'Pheromone diffuser' becomes 'the thing that stops the peeing.' If you cannot name 5 specific daily-life moments (the 11pm scrubbing, the 2am Googling, the bathroom anxiety on a date), you are not ready to apply this playbook. · 2. Borrow peer authority
Identify the grassroots community that quietly uses your product (shelters, foster groups, support forums, nurse groups, mom Facebook groups). Build testimonial-heavy creative featuring these peer voices, NOT PhDs or doctors. The authority reveal should come AFTER the viewer is hooked by their own recognized pain, not before. · 3. Show daily-life transformation
End every ad with the VIEWER imagining their life after the product works. Not 'our diffuser releases pheromones' but 'your house is quiet again. You took the crate apart. You stopped Googling at 2am.' Sell the after-state. Every transformation must be time-anchored (day 2, week 1, month 2) and behaviorally specific.
- **transferable:** Gut health supplements
- Gap: Every brand says 'supports digestive health.' No brand says 'you're at a coffee shop and you can't go and you're sweating.' The daily consequence language is absent.
- Authority: A nutritionist friend or a nurse who works 12-hour shifts — peer authority from someone who lives with the problem themselves.
- Transformation: 'I ate out on a date for the first time in months without worrying about my stomach.' Post-state described in lived-moment terms.

Menopause supplements
- Gap: Dominated by clinical language (Happy Mammoth, Peri). No brand says 'your partner noticed the mood changes before you did' or 'you can't remember the word for keys.'
- Authority: Perimenopause Facebook-group moderators, not endocrinologists. 'The woman who runs our midlife group told me about this.'
- Transformation: 'Her husband said she's back to herself.' Third-party validation of the recognizable-self state.
- **limits:** Selected sample: nine dog videos and one cat video, ranked by observed duration; no ad-level spend/reach/geography supplied. Related hook/footage variants do not establish independent pattern recurrence. Current dog creatives also invoke veterinary recommendation, which qualifies the legacy reference’s stronger peer-authority-only interpretation. Preserve the original reference as historical analysis. Cat-page FAQ contains dog wording; do not import that into the cat mechanism. Trial efficacy, claims and results are not independently tested in this pass.

MECHANISM EXPLANATION: show the hidden layer the old fix cannot reach with a 3D cutaway, then let the product reach it (#10, #11, #15, #29). CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE: let the child or the sufferer count the failed attempts ('burnt off seven times', #41) and say how it felt socially. MARKETING: keep 2 primary texts constant and test visuals under them; statics with a symptom question or a kid's handwritten sign ran for 700-940 days (#26, #28).

AI-made assets with garbled labels (#13, #17), a Veo watermark (one hemorrhoid clip), stock 'doctor' clips and an unverified '3-person experiment' (#46) and study claim (#20). The products are homeopathic (12C dilutions); the pages say uses are not evaluated by FDA or clinically tested. No spend or conversion data. Five of the Meta top 30 (all videos) had no downloadable file and are not included. Cart, checkout and upsells not observed.
