## Claim and fact checks to clear with the client before anything ships
1. **"Recommended by pediatricians"** (every set): the site never says it. Needs written proof (who, how many) or cut.
2. **Kids' formula vs the four plants:** the site says the **Kids 4+ oil has tea tree 15%, distilled lemon 5%, propolis
   5%, green-tea catechins 1%, and no menthol, eucalyptus or thuja oil**. White cedar *is* thuja, and pokeweed
   (Phytolacca 12C) is listed as an adult label active. So "kids' formula, ages 4 and up" + "pokeweed and white cedar
   break the wart down" describes two different products. Which bottle ships to kids, and what's in it?
3. **"Four plants"** leaves out propolis (bee-allergy warning on the site), green tea, and the adult eucalyptus/menthol.
4. **"Up to 8x deeper than a regular cream"**: keep the site's † footnote on screen; get the absorption test report.
5. **"No blister / no scar / gone in about two weeks / it never came back"**: results claims; the script marks them
   [proof needed]. AI testimonials must stay labelled.
6. **The study (van Haalen 2009, Br J Dermatol, 1,465 schoolkids)**: confirm the "about twice as likely" figure, and
   Set 19's "they found no link to the pool", against the paper before the citation card is made.
7. **"About half of kids' warts go away within a year"** (Set 18): needs a source line.
8. **Homeopathic actives** (Thuja 6X, Phytolacca 12C): the site's disclaimer ("not accepted medical evidence, not FDA
   evaluated") should ride on the end card wherever the plants "break the wart down".
9. **Set 17** puts a doctor figure on screen: "Dramatization", no name or credentials (as scripted), and no stacking with
   an unproven "recommended by pediatricians".
