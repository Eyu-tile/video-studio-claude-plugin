# Haven & Spool: what reviewers keep sending back

From 241 corrections strategists wrote on 139 briefs (`ads/REVIEW_NOTES.md`). Ranked by how often the theme comes
up. Read this before every edit and check each item before uploading.

1. **Product at the wrong size** (the most repeated note: #300, #355, #357, #395 ×2). The roll-on is a small
   **50 ml** amber bottle that sits in a palm. Generated shots make it "HUGE" or "way too small". Fix: prompt with
   a hand holding the bottle for scale and use the Products tab photos as reference images; check every product
   shot against them.
2. **Looks like AI** (#227, #357, #370, #291 ...): "not real enough", "looks like a studio shot", "a talking head the
   whole time looks fake". Fix: phone-shot, natural light, real skin texture; cover long AI talking heads with
   b-roll under the voice; regenerate anything that reads as AI. #457's rule for every shot: *"If AI didn't exist,
   how would a real brand film this?"*
3. **Not following the reference** (#366 "missing the whole first half of the video", #227 "use the exact same scenes
   as the reference", #302 "stop including the product when it's not in the reference"). For Duplicates: copy
   structure, scenes, timing, captions and music; add nothing the reference doesn't have.
4. **Wrong voice** (#386 changed a man's voice to a woman's; #297 mispronounced a year, 5 rounds; #422 suggests the
   ElevenLabs voice changer for a natural, consistent tone). Match the reference's gender, age and accent; listen
   to the whole VO for pronunciation (numbers, years, "Haven & Spool" = HAY-vin & SPOOL, ricinoleic =
   riss-in-oh-LAY-ik).
5. **Poor product application** (#332: "the way she's applying our product is really poor; use real-world product
   application footage as the motion reference"). Use the Library's real "Rolling on" clips or the Duplicates
   team's application shots as motion references.
6. **Glitches** (#309 "a blip", "it tweaks"; #266 "a white bar"). Watch every export frame by frame at the flagged
   moments before upload.
7. **Example people who don't sell the result** (#371 "she doesn't look that good to be used as an example";
   #336 "show someone with super clear smooth skin"). The "after" person must look like the promise.
8. **Copy changes the strategist makes late** (#307, #332, #423): apply them to *every* hook version, not just the
   one the note is on.
9. **Targeting words** (#423): say the angle's keyword in the hook ("Skin tags freezing kits vs ...") so Meta and the
   viewer see the topic immediately.
10. Small stuff that still costs a round: a ring on a presenter's finger (#415), header text too small (#331).

## Brand rules from briefs
- Castor seeds whole only; never show chewing, crushing or rubbing a raw seed on skin (#457).
- The product is the real bottle from the Products tab; it can't be invented or relabelled.
- Claims exactly as the brief writes them. Skin tag wording follows #156/#78 ("dries out and falls off"); the
  dermatologist lines carry "Source: American Academy of Dermatology".
- Respect the Meta safe zone (top 14%, bottom 35%, sides 6%).
